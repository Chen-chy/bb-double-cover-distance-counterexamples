# Binary strict-trinomial BB double covers: amplification round

## STATUS: CONSTANT_BOUND_STILL_OPEN

This report continues the supplied research baseline. It does not reclassify the degradation problem, and it does not assume that 7/3, 5/2, or 3 is a universal bound.

### Principal ledger

No finite universal upper bound on C_* is established in this round. The best rigorous lower bound retained is C_* >= 7/3. The highest exact BB amplification ratio certified here is 14/6 = 7/3; the supplied connected infinite family remains the record in this research program. None of the three proposed universal inequalities is proved or refuted here.

There are two new structural results derived in this round:

1. Every **univariate** strict-trinomial double cover turns any one-block base logical of weight w into some cover logical of weight at most 2w. More precisely, the corresponding one-block minimum either doubles exactly or has a weight-preserving upper bound, according to the subgroup generated by that polynomial's support differences. This holds for all period parities and term-matched changed-polynomial lifts.
2. Every nontrivial **split** strict-trinomial double cover has an explicit full-homology reduction to the half-size two-block complex (P^2, Q + delta P), delta in {0,1}. An exact four-term weight functional recovers the original cover distance. This reduction includes all cover logical sectors, not just transfer.

A tempting lemma suggested by the second result is false: the reduced code need not have distance at most the base distance. The certified 6 -> 14 example yields a reduced [[78,4,9]] code from a [[78,4,6]] base. This is **not a new BB amplification example**: the reduced second polynomial has four terms, and its distance is measured after a weight-distorting equivalence.

## 1. Inputs, scope, and exact computations

The supplied continuation brief is the source for the existing connected family



$$
[[252j+78,4,6]]\longrightarrow[[504j+156,4,14]],\qquad j\ge0,
$$



and for the previously established dimension, d=2, d=4, and size-dependent bounds. In particular, the input statements are



$$
k\le k_2\le2k,\quad k_2=2k\Rightarrow d_2\le2d,\quad
 d=2\Rightarrow d_2\le4,\quad d=4\Rightarrow d_2\le48.
$$



The previously disproved transfer-sector metric inequalities are also treated as input. No global upper bound is inferred from them.

The underlying cover convention is the term-matched period multiplication in Theorem 3.1 of Symons, Rajput, and Browne, *Sequences of Bivariate Bicycle Codes from Covering Graphs*, arXiv:2511.13560v3. The current paper was checked for context; the record family is attributed to the supplied research brief, not to the published paper.

For a finite abelian group G, the complex used below is



$$
R\xrightarrow{h\mapsto(Bh,Ah)}R^2
\xrightarrow{(u,v)\mapsto Au+Bv}R,
\qquad R=\mathbf F_2[G].
$$



A nonboundary cycle is a logical. Coordinate inversion and block exchange identify the X and Z distance problems, so exact exclusion in this complex gives the quantum distance. Explicitly, inversion conjugates each multiplication matrix to its transpose; block exchange then carries both cycles and boundaries to their dual counterparts.

All results below are labeled by their actual evidentiary type. The two structural theorems have uniform symbolic proofs. The finite reduced-code distance has exact exhaustive certificates. The parameter selection in the scans is a discovery procedure, not exhaustive coverage of all BB codes.

## 2. A sharp one-block theorem for univariate covers

Let a valid cover have periods L and 2L, with



$$
F=x^L+1,\quad R=\mathbf F_2[x]/(F),\quad
\widetilde R=\mathbf F_2[x]/(F^2),
$$



and term-matched strict trinomials A,B and A',B'. Define



$$
d_A=\min\{\operatorname{wt}(u): Au=0,\ (u,0)\text{ is a nonboundary}\}.
$$



The analogous quantity upstairs is d'_A. Both are finite when k>0; equivalently, the one-block logical quotient ann(A)/B ann(A) is nonzero. In the univariate ring its dimension is the degree of gcd(A,B,F).

Monomial gauges preserve weight and the complex. Normalize A' to



$$
A'=1+x^a+x^b,\qquad 0<a,b<2L,
$$



and put



$$
q=\gcd(a,b,L),\qquad q'=\gcd(a,b,2L).
$$



Then q' is either q or 2q.

**Theorem 1 (uniform symbolic).**



$$
\boxed{
q'=q\Rightarrow d'_A=2d_A,
\qquad
q'=2q\Rightarrow d'_A\le d_A.
}
$$



The same statement holds with the two blocks exchanged. In particular, if any minimum base logical is supported in one block, then



$$
\boxed{d_2\le2d.}
$$



More generally, any one-block base logical of weight w gives d_2 <= 2w, whether or not it is globally minimum. No assumption k_2=2k is needed.

### 2.1 A trinomial multiplicity fact

Write



$$
a=2^r\alpha,\qquad b=2^r\beta,
$$



where at least one of alpha,beta is odd. Then



$$
1+x^a+x^b=(1+x^\alpha+x^\beta)^{2^r}.
$$



The polynomial in parentheses is square-free. If exactly one exponent is odd, its derivative is a monomial and is nonzero at every nonzero root. If both are odd, a zero of its derivative would satisfy x^alpha=x^beta, making the trinomial evaluate to 1. Thus every nonzero root of A' has multiplicity exactly 2^r.

Every root of F has multiplicity f=2^{v_2(L)}. The equality q'=q is equivalent to r <= v_2(L). Therefore, in that case,



$$
\gcd(A',F^2)=\gcd(A',F)=\gcd(A,F)=:g_A.
$$



### 2.2 Connected support lift: exact doubling

The annihilator ideals are



$$
\operatorname{ann}_R(A)=(F/g_A),\qquad
\operatorname{ann}_{\widetilde R}(A')=(F^2/g_A)
 =F\operatorname{ann}_R(A).
$$



Consequently, multiplication by F is an isomorphism of vector spaces



$$
\tau:\operatorname{ann}_R(A)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{ann}_{\widetilde R}(A').
$$



It intertwines B and B', because B' is congruent to B modulo F. It therefore induces an isomorphism



$$
\operatorname{ann}(A)/B\operatorname{ann}(A)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{ann}(A')/B'\operatorname{ann}(A').
$$



For a representative u of degree below L, the two halves of Fu=u+x^L u are disjoint, so wt(Fu)=2 wt(u). Every one-block cover cycle has this form. The equality d'_A=2d_A follows.

This is injection on the relevant **one-block logical quotient**, not an assertion that transfer is injective on all H_1.

### 2.3 Disconnected support lift: a short projected logical

Let H be the subgroup of C_L generated by a and b, and H' the subgroup of C_{2L} generated by their actual cover exponents. When q'=2q, both have size L/q and the projection H' -> H is an isomorphism.

Decompose a one-block logical u into its restrictions u_C to cosets C of H. Since every normalized A term lies in H, every u_C is an A-cycle. At least one (u_C,0) is a nonboundary; otherwise their sum would be a boundary.

Choose a lift of that coset. The isomorphism H' -> H lifts u_C to an A'-cycle with exactly the same weight. Its projection is the base nonboundary, so it cannot be a cover boundary. This proves d'_A <= d_A.

The argument imposes no restriction on the support of B'. It uses only its compatibility with the full BB projection chain map.

### 2.4 Scope and limitation

The result is univariate, not a general bivariate theorem. Its proof depends on the uniform root multiplicity of a binary trinomial and the cyclic support subgroup.

It improves the prior fixed-weight one-block obstruction to a relative factor-two bound in this setting. It does not establish a bound in terms of the global distance when every minimum logical is mixed-block. The ratio min(d_A,d_B)/d need not be bounded by the results used here.

Finite checks in `verify_theorems.py` exercise 372 term-matched examples, including 317 exact-doubling and 55 weight-preserving support cases. They check annihilator and boundary-subspace identities; these finite checks supplement, rather than replace, the proof.

## 3. A full-homology reduction for split double covers

Suppose the group extension splits:



$$
\widetilde G\cong G\times C_2,
\qquad \widetilde R=R[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2),
\quad \epsilon=1+t.
$$



This includes period doubling in an odd coordinate. The other coordinate may be even; semisimplicity of R is not assumed.

Each lifted trinomial has a majority sheet. Multiplication by the deck monomial t interchanges the two sheets, so a monomial gauge leaves at most one term on the minority sheet. Thus each perturbation coefficient in epsilon coordinates is either zero or a monomial.

If both perturbations are zero, the cover is equivalent to two disjoint base copies: k_2=2k and d_2=d. Otherwise exchange blocks if necessary and multiply by base monomial units. The pair takes the form



$$
\boxed{A'=P+\epsilon,\qquad B'=Q+\delta\epsilon,
\qquad\delta\in\{0,1\}.}
$$



The base pair (P,Q) is permutation-equivalent to the original one. P contains the identity term; Q can also be normalized to contain it. For delta=1 this normalization comes from its minority monomial.

Set



$$
D=Q+\delta P,
\qquad E=C(P^2,D;R).
$$



The reduced complex has 2|G| qubits, half the number upstairs. When delta=1, D has at most four terms and need not be a strict trinomial. This change of class must not be hidden.

**Theorem 2 (uniform symbolic).** There is an explicit isomorphism



$$
\boxed{H_1(C(A',B';\widetilde R))\cong H_1(E).}
$$



It includes every cover homology class, whether or not the class comes from transfer.

### 3.1 Exact cycle and boundary maps

Write a cover cycle as



$$
U=u_0+\epsilon u_1,\qquad V=v_0+\epsilon v_1.
$$



Its two equations are



$$
Pu_0+Qv_0=0,
\qquad
u_0+Pu_1+\delta v_0+Qv_1=0.
$$



Define



$$
r=u_1+\delta v_1,
\qquad s=v_0+Pv_1.
$$



Then



$$
P^2r+Ds=0.
$$



A cover boundary generated by h_0+epsilon h_1 maps exactly to



$$
(Dh_1,P^2h_1),
$$



a boundary of E.

Conversely, a reduced cycle (r,s) has the lift



$$
\mathcal L(r,s)=\bigl(Pr+\delta s+\epsilon r,\ s\bigr).
$$



The reduction of this lift is (r,s). An arbitrary cover cycle differs from the lift of its reduction by the cover boundary generated by v_1. Therefore the induced maps are mutually inverse on H_1.

These identities hold over an arbitrary commutative coefficient ring R of characteristic two. No domain, square-free-period, or homological-sector assumption is used.

### 3.2 An exact physical-weight formula

The actual group basis is {1,t}, not {1,epsilon}. In physical sheet coordinates the canonical lift has components



$$
\bigl((P+1)r+\delta s,\ r,\ s,\ 0\bigr).
$$



A further boundary generated by h in R gives the four components



$$
\begin{aligned}
&(P+1)r+\delta s+(Q+\delta)h,\\
&r+\delta h,\\
&s+(P+1)h,\\
&h.
\end{aligned}
$$



Let W_delta(r,s,h) be the sum of their four Hamming weights. Then the original cover distance is exactly



$$
\boxed{
 d_2=\min_{\substack{P^2r+Ds=0\\[(r,s)]\ne0\ \mathrm{in}\ H_1(E)}}
 \ \min_{h\in R} W_\delta(r,s,h).
}
$$



All reduced-cycle representatives are allowed in the outer minimum. Hence reduced boundaries, corresponding to the h_1 part of a cover boundary, have already been included. The inner h is the remaining h_0 freedom. This explains why the displayed minimum is over all physical cover representatives, not only canonical lifts.

Since P+1 has weight two,



$$
d_2\le\min_{[(r,s)]\ne0}
\bigl(3\operatorname{wt}(r)+(1+\delta)\operatorname{wt}(s)\bigr)
\le3d(E).
$$



For physical coordinates (U_0,U_1,V_0,V_1), the reduction is



$$
(r,s)=(U_1+\delta V_1,\ V_0+(P+1)V_1),
$$



so its weight is at most (2+delta) times the original weight. Thus



$$
\boxed{\frac{d(E)}{2+\delta}\le d_2\le3d(E).}
$$



Neither inequality compares d(E) to the original base distance. Such a comparison is an additional mathematical problem.

The implementation checks 192 finite split instances, including even coefficient-ring periods and 46 positive-k cases. It verifies the full cycle and boundary maps and the weight inequalities.

## 4. A disproved shortcut: the reduced distance can increase

The candidate lemma



$$
d(C(P^2,Q+\delta P))\le d(C(P,Q))
$$



would have combined with Theorem 2 to prove a factor-three bound for split covers. It is false.

Take R=F_2[x]/(x^39+1) and



$$
P=1+x^{14}+x^{22},\qquad Q=1+x^{26}+x^{28},\quad\delta=1.
$$



These are monomial gauges of the known base polynomials:



$$
P=x^{-25}(1+x^8+x^{25}),\qquad
Q=x^{-13}(1+x^2+x^{13})\pmod{x^{39}+1}.
$$



Therefore the base is [[78,4,6]]. Under the split-coordinate identification of its known double cover, the cover pair is P+epsilon,Q+epsilon.

The reduced pair is



$$
\boxed{A_E=1+x^5+x^{28},\qquad
 B_E=x^{14}+x^{22}+x^{26}+x^{28}.}
$$



It has exact parameters



$$
\boxed{E=[[78,4,9]].}
$$



A weight-nine logical has



$$
\operatorname{supp}(r)=\{0,5,10,11,16,28\},\qquad
\operatorname{supp}(s)=\{24,30,32\}.
$$



The ordinary and period gcds are both 1+x+x^2. Exact polynomial normal-form evaluation verifies nontriviality. The independent matrix test agrees.

For the lower bound, `mitm.cpp` generates all 1,505,583 subsets of the 78 columns of size at most four and sorts them by syndrome. Every cycle of weight at most eight is a difference of two such subsets with equal syndrome. There are exactly 1,365 equal-syndrome pairs, yielding exactly 39 distinct nonzero cycles. All have weight seven and are boundaries; they are the translates of the single-check boundary (B_E,A_E).

Thus no logical of weight at most eight exists. A separate circuit search and a separate Python polynomial-oracle search reach the same exclusion.

This proves the failure 9>6 without making a new amplification claim. In particular, 9/6 is not being entered into the BB-cover record table. The actual cover here still has distance fourteen.

The exact weight functional also explains the failure of a naive replacement of cover distance by the reduced Hamming distance: the reduction is a homology isomorphism, not a weight-preserving permutation.

### 5.3 Template selection

The discovery procedure enumerated normalized trinomials A of degree at most 45 and trinomial multiples B of degree at most four times deg(A). This degree-box calculation is not a classification of all trinomial identities. Its boundary is explicit, and it is not extrapolated to an infinite optimality theorem. It rediscovered the known seven-term quotient mechanism, not a larger-ratio family.

## 6. Exactness of the distance engine

A minimum nonboundary cycle has circuit support: recursively split a nonminimal dependent support into two disjoint cycles; if both were boundaries, their sum would be a boundary. Translation permits a root at left column zero whenever the circuit uses the left block. Pure-right circuits are covered by a root at right column zero with left columns forbidden.

At a nonzero syndrome, choose an odd check row. Every completion to a cycle must select at least one still-available incident column. Branches are partitioned by the first selected column in an ordered list; earlier alternatives are forbidden in later branches. This preserves a branch for every target circuit.

A zero syndrome terminates the branch after testing logical nontriviality. A circuit cannot contain a proper nonempty zero-syndrome support, so this stopping rule does not remove an unvisited circuit completion.

The new pruning rule packs odd rows with disjoint **available-column** neighborhoods. Any completion must select a different new column for each packed row, giving a rigorous lower bound on its remaining weight. This is a certificate-preserving lower bound, not a decoder heuristic.

The C++ implementation uses binary Gaussian elimination for boundary membership. The independent Python implementation uses Bezout polynomial normal-form coordinates. On the retained exact examples it excludes base weights through four, seed-cover weights through twelve, and reduced-code weights through eight. The two circuit implementations use different row and column orders. A meet-in-the-middle exclusion additionally verifies the reduced code independently of circuit branching.

The engines also agree with brute-force subset enumeration on 45 small strict and non-strict two-block instances. No floating-point rank test or decoder estimate is used.

A node budget is available only for discovery. Its output contains an explicit cutoff flag. A result with cutoff=true and found=false is never a lower bound. Final exact exclusions use zero budget, meaning no cutoff.

## 8. Consequences for the central extremal question

The best rigorous ledger remains



$$
\boxed{C_*\ge7/3,\qquad\text{no finite universal numerical upper bound established}.}
$$



For the truncated supremum Gamma(N), the supplied O(N^{3/4}) upper bound remains available. A size-dependent bound on Gamma(N) must not be reported as a finite bound on C_*.

The one-block theorem removes a broader univariate mechanism than the previous fixed-weight estimate: a counterexample above two must have globally minimum logicals that are genuinely mixed-block. It does not force those mixed logicals to have any particular bounded weight.

The split reduction supplies a complete algebraic and metric description of every cover class in a large regime. However, neither its unweighted distance nor the transfer-sector metric is automatically controlled by the base minimum. The exact 6 -> 9 reduced-code counterexample invalidates one particularly natural attempted proof of the factor-three milestone.

**Smallest explicit obstruction in this route.** For a minimum mixed base cycle, it remains unknown how to find a nonzero reduced class (r,s) and boundary adjustment h with W_delta(r,s,h) bounded by a universal constant times the base weight. Restricting to transfer misses classes; restricting to h=0 loses weight-reducing cover boundaries; replacing W_delta by reduced Hamming weight leads to the disproved comparison above.

**Single next mathematical target.** Prove or refute the factor-three inequality in this explicit physical norm for the transformation



$$
(P,Q)\longmapsto(P^2,Q+\delta P),\qquad\delta\in\{0,1\},
$$



starting with delta=1 and mixed base minima. A proof would settle the entire split-cover case, including all odd-period univariate doubles and odd-coordinate bivariate doubles. A counterexample must be certified in W_delta, not merely in the reduced Hamming metric. Non-split even-coordinate covers would still require a separate argument for a universal theorem.
