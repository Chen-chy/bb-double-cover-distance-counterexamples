# Compact survival for strict-trinomial BB covers

## STATUS: GENERAL_FIXED_DISTANCE_BOUND_PROVED

Research round: 6 September 2026.

This report continues the supplied binary strict-trinomial BB amplification problem. The proofs below do not enumerate the eight-column presentations, and do not require separate classifications at weights 8, 10, 12, and so on.

### Principal conclusions

1. **Higher-rank compact survival.** For every incidence presentation generated by its four check differences, of free rank at least two, with neither check supported in a finite subgroup, a surviving compact base class forces a surviving compact universal-cover class. This holds at arbitrary circuit weight. In fact, one compact cover cycle can be fixed before choosing the finite quotient and works for every quotient in which some compact base class survives.
2. **A general fixed-distance bound.** Every valid binary strict-trinomial BB double cover with positive base dimension satisfies
   

$$
d_2\le\max\{2d^4,\;8d^3,\;4d^{\log_2 9}\}.
$$


   In particular, $d_2\le2d^4$ for $d\ge4$. Combining with the already established $d=2\Rightarrow d_2\le4$,
   

$$
\boxed{d_2\le2d^4\quad\text{for all valid covers}.}
$$


   Without importing even the previous distance-two result, the displayed maximum gives the standalone bound $d_2\le4d^4$.
3. **Geometric refinements.** Combining the new structural argument with the published arbitrary-boundary geometric distance theorem gives
   

$$
\boxed{d_2\le\max\{32d^3,\;4d^{\log_2 9}\}
   \le32d^{\log_2 9},\qquad \log_2 9\approx3.169925.}
$$


   A separate rank-sensitive use of that theorem gives
   

$$
\boxed{\Gamma(N)=O(\sqrt N).}
$$


   An intentionally unoptimized explicit constant is $\Gamma(N)\le24\sqrt N$.
4. **Consequences and limits.** No family can have bounded base distance and diverging cover distance. An unbounded amplification ratio, if possible, must have base distance tending to infinity along a subsequence. None of these polynomial bounds implies a finite constant $C_*$. The supplied record $7/3$ and the statuses of the constant thresholds $7/3,5/2,3$ are unchanged.

The new compact-survival argument is a symbolic proof. The arithmetic and finite-code checks in the bundle are supplementary verification, not a substitute for the proof or an exhaustive classification. No proof-assistant formalization or literature-priority claim is made.

## 1. Precise setting

For a finitely generated abelian group $U$, let $S=\mathbf F_2[U]$, with its monomial basis. The universal BB complex is


$$
S\xrightarrow{f\mapsto(Bf,Af)}S^2
\xrightarrow{(u,v)\mapsto Au+Bv}S.
$$


The checks are sums of three distinct group elements. After monomial gauges, one term of each is the identity. We require that their four remaining group elements generate $U$. This is exactly the condition obtained by eliminating column positions from a connected incidence presentation.

A universal or compact cycle is an element of this complex over $S$, hence has finite support. A finite quotient $U\twoheadrightarrow G$ specializes the complex to $\mathbf F_2[G]$. A compatible universal double cover is an extension


$$
0\longrightarrow C_2=\langle t\rangle
\longrightarrow\widetilde U\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow0
$$


with term-matched lifted checks. A finite quotient double cover must preserve the nonidentity deck element. We allow arbitrary additional quotient relations, torsion collapse, nonsplit finite covers, and even periods.

The principal survival theorem concerns the remaining higher-rank regime: free rank at least two, and neither check contained in a coset of a finite subgroup. The finite-subgroup cases have a direct uniform distance bound and are handled separately. We do not assert an unrestricted compact-survival theorem for arbitrary Koszul pairs or every low-rank presentation.

The standard BB identification of X and Z distances, and the interpretation of nonboundaries as logicals, are part of the supplied baseline. All subsequent universal-versus-finite arguments are stated explicitly.

## 2. Why a general base-change argument would be invalid

Finite generation of $H_1$ does not make its specialization survive. For a free complex $C$, the relevant spectral sequence has


$$
E^2_{p,q}=\operatorname{Tor}^S_p(H_q(C),S/I)
\Longrightarrow H_{p+q}(C\otimes_S S/I).
$$


In particular the compact edge map


$$
H_1(C)\otimes_S S/I\longrightarrow H_1(C\otimes_S S/I)
$$


need not be injective, and new homology can come from $\operatorname{Tor}_1(H_0(C),S/I)$. This is the standard derived-tensor spectral sequence; see Stacks Project, Tags 061Y and 061Z.

Here is an explicit negative control, outside the strict-trinomial BB class. Set $S=\mathbf F_2[g,c]$. The base pair is


$$
A=g,\qquad B=g(1+c).
$$


Its universal first homology is $S/(g)$, generated by $(1+c,1)$. Over $S[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, take


$$
A'=g+\epsilon,\qquad B'=g(1+c)+\epsilon.
$$


The usual elimination of $\epsilon$ identifies its universal first homology with that of the pair $(g^2,gc)$ over $S$. Consequently it is $S/(g)$, with a generator represented in the cover by


$$
\bigl(g(1+c)+\epsilon c,\;g\bigr).
$$


At $g=c=0$, the base generator becomes $(1,1)$ and is nontrivial, while every universal cover class maps to zero. The finite cover pair is $(\epsilon,\epsilon)$, whose first homology has dimension two.

Thus even the implication "some compact base class survives, therefore some compact cover class survives" is false for unrestricted square-zero Koszul deformations. Here


$$
H_1(C)\otimes_S\mathbf F_2\cong\mathbf F_2
$$


is nonzero but its edge image is zero. The cover's $H_0$, regarded as an $S$-module, is $S/(g^2,gc)$. Its resolution has last syzygy $(c,g)$, so $\operatorname{Tor}_2(H_0,\mathbf F_2)\cong\mathbf F_2$; the transgression kills the compact edge term. This is not a BB counterexample.

The argument below avoids this issue by proving an explicit generator identity in the universal base homology first, and then proving a cover nonboundary directly in the actual finite ring.

## 3. Noncollinear Laurent trinomials have only Frobenius factorization

**Lemma 1.** Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two, a Laurent trinomial with noncollinear exponents is a monomial unit times a $2^a$-th power of an irreducible Laurent trinomial. After removing the largest common power of two from its exponent differences, the remaining trinomial is irreducible.

### Proof

First work in the saturated rank-two lattice spanned by the exponent differences; it is a direct summand of the ambient free lattice. Adjoining the other Laurent variables does not change irreducibility. After a unimodular coordinate change, multiplication by a monomial, and scaling, it suffices to consider


$$
1+\lambda x^a+\mu x^b y^c,
\qquad a,c>0,\quad b\in\mathbf Z.
$$


Remove the largest common power of two dividing $a,b,c$, taking the unique corresponding roots of the coefficients. Assume now that $a,b,c$ are not all even.

Over $K(x)$, a root $y$ satisfies


$$
y^c=\mu^{-1}x^{-b}(1+\lambda x^a)=:f(x).
$$


Let $N\le c$ be the degree of the field extension generated by one root of an irreducible factor. Norms give


$$
\operatorname{Nm}(y)^c=f(x)^N.
$$


At zero, the valuation of $f$ is $-b$. At any root of $1+\lambda x^a$, its valuation is $2^{v_2(a)}$. Since the norm lies in $K(x)$,


$$
c\mid Nb,\qquad c\mid N2^{v_2(a)}.
$$


But


$$
\gcd(c,b,2^{v_2(a)})=1.
$$


Therefore $c\mid N$, so $N=c$. This proves irreducibility, including the inseparable cases. Gauss's lemma and Laurent localization complete the proof. $\square$

**Corollary.** Two noncollinear Laurent trinomials sharing a nonunit factor have, after monomial gauges and possibly exchanging them, matching supports and coefficients under a Frobenius power:


$$
\overline B=\overline A^{q},\qquad q=2^s.
$$


A collinear Laurent polynomial cannot share a factor with a noncollinear trinomial: over the algebraic closure its factors are binomials in one primitive monomial direction. Two collinear checks in independent directions are likewise coprime.

## 4. Symbolic classification of every genuinely higher-rank compact mechanism

**Theorem 2 (normal form).** Suppose $U$ is generated by the four normalized check differences, has free rank at least two, neither check has finite support subgroup, and


$$
H_1(C(A,B;\mathbf F_2[U]))\ne0.
$$


Then $U$ has free rank exactly two. After monomial gauges and block exchange,


$$
\boxed{
U=\langle X,Y\rangle\oplus T\cong\mathbf Z^2\oplus T,
\quad A=1+X+Y,
\quad B=1+hX^q+kY^q,
\quad q=2^s,
\quad T=\langle h,k\rangle.
}
$$


In particular the torsion group is generated by at most two elements. This is a mechanism classification for arbitrary compact weight, not an enumeration at weight eight.

### Proof

Write the torsion subgroup as $T_{\rm odd}\oplus T_2$, and extend constants to an algebraic closure. This extension is faithfully flat. Split the odd-order group algebra into its character components, then reduce each component modulo the nilpotent augmentation ideal of its two-primary group algebra.

At least one character component has nonzero universal $H_1$. If the two residual Laurent polynomials were a regular sequence, the original component would have vanishing positive Koszul homology. One may see this directly by using the finite nilpotent filtration: the associated graded complex is the residual Koszul complex tensored with vector spaces, and exactness lifts one filtration level at a time. Thus the two residual polynomials must share a nonunit factor, unless one is zero.

A residual trinomial can be zero only if all of its free exponents coincide; this is the excluded finite-subgroup case. A surviving residual monomial is a unit and also gives no homology. In all other cases, the free support and Lemma 1 show that a nonzero common factor at free rank at least two requires both checks to be noncollinear and their exponent triangles to be Frobenius translates. Collinear checks with a common factor would generate at most one free direction.

Normalize the lower Frobenius triangle as $\{0,a_1,a_2\}$. The other normalized support is, after choosing which of its terms becomes the identity, $\{0,qa_1,qa_2\}$ modulo torsion. Since all four support differences generate the free quotient of $U$, the images of $a_1,a_2$ are a basis of that quotient. Let their actual monomials be $X,Y$. The other two terms are uniquely $hX^q,kY^q$ for torsion elements $h,k$. They generate all the torsion, because $X,Y,h,k$ generate $U$ and $X,Y$ are a free basis. This proves the normal form. $\square$

An immediate additional consequence is that a connected universal two-trinomial complex of free rank at least three has no compact first homology. Its finite homology can still be nonzero, but then those classes are created by specialization. A short finite circuit necessarily imposes enough incidence relations to lower the free rank of its own presentation.

## 5. The full universal compact module is cyclic

Let the checks have the normal form of Theorem 2, and put


$$
e=\sum_{t\in T_{\rm odd}}t.
$$


This is an idempotent. First,


$$
\boxed{H_1=eH_1.}
$$


To see this, use $A=1+X+Y$, a non-zero-divisor, to identify


$$
H_1\cong\operatorname{ann}_{S/(A)}(B).
$$


Here


$$
S/(A)\cong\mathbf F_2[T][X^{\pm1},(1+X)^{\pm1}].
$$


In an odd character $\chi$, the residual polynomial of $B$ is


$$
(1+\chi(k))+(\chi(h)+\chi(k))X^q.
$$


It is nonzero unless $\chi(h)=\chi(k)=1$. A nonzero residual polynomial is a non-zero-divisor by the nilpotent filtration argument. Since $h,k$ generate $T$, only the trivial odd character contributes to $H_1$.

Consequently the entire compact first homology is the odd-norm inclusion of the complex on $\overline U=U/T_{\rm odd}$. Work now over this quotient, so $T$ is a two-primary group generated by $h,k$. Let


$$
M=\exp(T),\qquad M=2^a.
$$


The convention for the trivial group is $M=1$.

### The nilpotent-pencil lemma

Put $\mathcal B=\mathbf F_2[X^{\pm1},(1+X)^{\pm1}]$,


$$
\alpha=X^q,\qquad\beta=(1+X)^q,
\qquad\alpha+\beta=1,
$$


and


$$
D=\alpha(1+h)+\beta(1+k)\in\mathcal B[T].
$$


Then


$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{ann}(D)=D^{M-1}\mathcal B[T],
\qquad
\operatorname{ann}(D^{M-1})=D\mathcal B[T].
}
$$


For $M=1$, interpret $D^0=1$, and both statements have their obvious meaning.

Choose one of $h,k$ of maximal order $M$, say $h$. Such a generator exists because they generate $T$. A maximal-order element of a finite abelian two-group is a cyclic direct summand; since the quotient is cyclic, write


$$
T=\langle h\rangle\times\langle w\rangle,
\qquad |w|=m\le M,
\qquad k=h^j w.
$$


With $u=1+h$, $v=1+w$,


$$
\mathcal B[T]=\mathcal B[u,v]/(u^M,v^m).
$$


The coefficient of $u$ in the linear part of $D$ is either $\alpha$ or $\alpha+\beta=1$, hence is a unit. Moreover $D^M=0$. The substitution


$$
(u,v)\longmapsto(D,v)
$$


is an automorphism: its linear part is invertible, and the higher terms can be inverted successively in the nilpotent ideal $(u,v)$. Equivalently it gives a surjection between two free $\mathcal B$-modules of rank $Mm$, and the inverse is obtained by finite nilpotent iteration. Thus the algebra is also


$$
\mathcal B[D,v]/(D^M,v^m).
$$


Both annihilator identities follow. This proof holds over the whole coefficient ring $\mathcal B$, not only over its fraction field or at generic points.

### Generator theorem

The check identity is


$$
B^M=A^{qM}.
$$


Define the canonical power cycle


$$
\boxed{Z=(A^{qM-1},B^{M-1}).}
$$


Because $A$ is a non-zero-divisor, the map sending a cycle $(u,v)$ to $v\bmod A$ gives


$$
H_1\cong\operatorname{ann}_{S/(A)}(B).
$$


The generator's second coordinate reduces to $D^{M-1}$. The pencil lemma therefore proves


$$
\boxed{H_1(C(A,B;\mathbf F_2[\overline U]))
\cong \mathbf F_2[\overline U]/(A,B),\quad 1\longmapsto[Z].}
$$


In particular, **every compact cycle is a multiple of $Z$, modulo a universal boundary**. This statement is established before imposing any finite quotient relations.

Restoring the odd subgroup gives a single odd-norm generator for the entire original compact module. There can still be additional homology after finite specialization, including in nontrivial odd-character sectors. The generator statement does not identify universal and specialized homology.

## 6. Compact survival in every compatible finite double cover

**Theorem 3 (compact survival).** In the regime of Theorem 2, fix any universal abelian double cover with matched trinomial lifts. There is a compact cover cycle, depending only on that universal cover, which survives in every compatible finite quotient for which the image of compact base homology is nonzero.

An explicit bound on its weight is


$$
\boxed{2|T_{\rm odd}|\bigl((qM)^{\log_2 3}+M^{\log_2 3}\bigr),
\quad M=\exp(T_2).}
$$



### Odd reduction is valid even when torsion collapses

Let a compact base cycle survive in a finite quotient. Since $H_1=eH_1$, its finite class equals its odd-norm projection. The image of $e$ is exactly the norm of the image odd subgroup: every fiber has odd cardinality. Coset-sum inclusion from the quotient by that image subgroup has projection as a left inverse, hence injects on homology. The projected base cycle is therefore nontrivial.

Its universal quotient class is a multiple of $Z$. Thus $Z$ itself is a nonboundary in the actual finite quotient code. If $Z$ were a finite boundary, so would be every specialized multiple and hence the selected cycle.

The odd subgroup has a unique odd-order lift in the universal cover. Quotienting by it gives a double cover of $\overline U$. The deck element cannot be lost in an odd subgroup.

### An actual finite nonboundary from the power relation

Here is the needed lifting argument, included to make the survival proof self-contained. Let $m,n$ be powers of two, suppose $A^m=B^n$, and suppose $(A^{m-1},B^{n-1})$ is a finite base nonboundary. Put $\epsilon=1+t$ upstairs.

The lifted powers obey


$$
B'^n+A'^m=\epsilon D,
$$


where $D$ is a subset of the three terms of $A'^m$. The terms remain distinct in every relevant finite quotient: a collision would turn a three-term Frobenius power into a monomial unit, making the base code acyclic.

If $D=0$, the compact cycle $(A'^{m-1},B'^{n-1})$ projects to the base nonboundary. If $D=A'^m$, the compact cycle $(tA'^{m-1},B'^{n-1})$ does so.

Otherwise take


$$
W=(\epsilon A'^{m-1},\epsilon B'^{n-1}).
$$


If $W=(B'f,A'f)$ were a finite boundary, the two equations imply


$$
\epsilon Df=0,\qquad\epsilon A'^m f=0.
$$


One of $D,D+A'^m$ is a monomial unit. Hence $\epsilon f=0$. In an actual group-algebra double cover,


$$
\operatorname{ann}(\epsilon)=\epsilon R',
$$


so $f=\epsilon r$. Projecting the boundary equations after cancelling the transfer gives


$$
B\,p(r)=A^{m-1},\qquad A\,p(r)=B^{n-1},
$$


contradicting the base nonboundary.

For $m=2^a,n=2^b$, the cover weight is at most $2(3^a+3^b)$. Apply this with $m=qM,n=M$, then take the odd coset sum back upstairs. The latter injects finite homology and multiplies weight by at most $|T_{\rm odd}|$.

All branch choices can be made in the universal cover. The same resulting compact cycle works after every compatible finite quotient having nonzero compact base image. No base-change injectivity theorem is assumed, and no winding class is required to establish survival. $\square$

This rules out the proposed parametric compact-annihilation countermechanism in every genuinely higher-rank non-finite-check presentation, at any compact weight.

## 7. A uniform presentation-size estimate from one minimum logical

Let a finite base code have distance $d$, and choose a weight-$d$ nonboundary. Its support is a circuit: if it contained a proper cycle, that cycle or its complementary cycle would give a smaller nonboundary. Every cycle has even weight, since every column has odd weight three.

Pair the incidences at each touched check. The resulting loopless cubic graph on $d$ selected columns is connected. Any disconnected pairing component would be a proper cycle. Choose a spanning tree and eliminate the $d-1$ relative column positions. The remaining


$$
3d/2-(d-1)=d/2+1
$$


relations lie in $\mathbf Z^4$, on the two normalized differences of each check. They present a group $U$ mapping onto the connected base component. The original logical is a compact cycle over $\mathbf F_2[U]$, and it cannot be a universal boundary because it survives in the finite code. Any forced equality of check terms or of two selected same-block positions in $U$ would persist in the finite quotient and contradict the finite input. Thus the universal checks remain strict and the distinguished cycle retains weight $d$.

Define the root polytope


$$
\mathcal P=\operatorname{conv}\{
\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm(e_1-e_2),
\pm e_3,\pm e_4,\pm(e_3-e_4)\}.
$$


A fundamental graph cycle has length at most $d$. At each visited column it contributes a difference of two of that column's three labels, one of the displayed roots. Therefore every relation row belongs to $d\mathcal P$.

Every square minor of the root configuration has absolute value at most one. For a four-by-four determinant this is immediate: a nonzero choice has two roots in each two-dimensional block, each block determinant being $\pm1$. The lower-dimensional minors follow in the same way. By multilinearity and convexity, every $j$-minor of relation rows has absolute value at most $d^j$.

Write $r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbf Z}U$ for the free rank and $T$ for its torsion subgroup.

### Rank zero

Smith normal form, or the index of the relation lattice, gives


$$
|U|\le d^4.
$$


The cover of the connected component has at most $2d^4$ group elements. A positive-k BB code on a finite group of size $s$ has distance at most $s$, so


$$
d_2\le2d^4.
$$



### Rank one

Choose three independent relation rows. Let $c\in\mathbf Z^4$ be their signed cofactor vector, $g$ the gcd of its entries, and $v=c/g$ a primitive free-coordinate vector. The full torsion order divides $g$.

For any root $\ell$ corresponding to a difference between two terms of one check,


$$
|T|\,|\ell(v)|\le|\ell(c)|
=\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}\text{three relation rows}\\\ell\end{pmatrix}\right|
\le d^3.
$$


Thus, if $\rho$ is the larger exponent span of the two checks in that free coordinate,


$$
|T|\rho\le d^3.
$$


A cover is a cyclic strip with at most $2|T|$ group elements per site and range $\rho$, giving


$$
d_2\le8|T|\rho\le8d^3.
$$



### Rank at least two, one check in a finite subgroup

Its support subgroup is contained in the torsion of $U$. The minor bound gives


$$
|T|\le d^{4-r}\le d^2,
$$


so the finite-subgroup bound gives $d_2\le2d^2$.

### Remaining higher rank

The normal-form theorem applies, so $r=2$, and


$$
A=1+X+Y,\qquad B=1+hX^q+kY^q.
$$


In the original normalized coordinates, the free support of the second triangle is $q$ times a unimodular reorientation of the first. The reorientation matrix is one of the matrices induced by choosing a different vertex of a triangle as its origin and permuting its remaining vertices; its determinant is $\pm1$.

Write each relation row as $(a,b)$, with two entries from each check. Then $a=-qMb$ for one fixed unimodular matrix $M$, up to the harmless row/column convention. Projection onto the $b$-coordinates identifies the saturated relation lattice with $\mathbf Z^2$. Its sublattice index is precisely $|T|$. For two independent relation rows,


$$
q^2|T|\le |\det(a_1,a_2)|\le d^2.
$$


Therefore


$$
\boxed{q^2|T|\le d^2.}
$$



Put $\alpha=\log_2 3$, $h=|T_{\rm odd}|$, and $M=\exp(T_2)$. Since $hM\le|T|$, the compact-survival weight bound satisfies


$$
\begin{aligned}
d_2&\le2hM^\alpha(q^\alpha+1)\\
&\le2|T|^\alpha(q^\alpha+1)\\
&\le2d^{2\alpha}(q^{-\alpha}+q^{-2\alpha})\\
&\le4d^{2\alpha}=4d^{\log_2 9}.
\end{aligned}
$$



These four cases prove the general bound in the opening statement. There is no unhandled weight-eight case and no need to enumerate larger circuits.

## 8. The two elementary finite-code bounds used above

### Finite support subgroup

If $A$ lies in $\mathbf F_2[H]$, $|H|=s$, then $\operatorname{ann}(A)$ is spanned by vectors supported in individual $H$-cosets, of weight at most $s$. Its one-block logical quotient is


$$
\operatorname{ann}(A)/B\operatorname{ann}(A).
$$


The dimension of this cokernel equals the dimension of the kernel of multiplication by $B$ on $\operatorname{ann}(A)$, namely the common annihilator of $A,B$. The nondegenerate identity-coefficient pairing on a finite group algebra identifies that common annihilator with the dual of $R/(A,B)$. Thus the quotient is nonzero when $k>0$, and at least one local spanning vector survives. Consequently $d\le s$. In a double cover, the preimage subgroup has size $2s$.

### Cyclic strip

A code with $h$ group elements per cyclic site and check-support diameter $\rho$ satisfies $d\le4h\rho$. For a circle shorter than $2\rho$, total size suffices. Otherwise partition the sites into an even number of consecutive blocks, each between $\rho$ and $2\rho$ sites long. Such a partition is obtained using $2\lfloor L/(2\rho)\rfloor$ blocks and distributing sites evenly.

If $d>4h\rho$, every block is correctable. No generator meets two distinct blocks of the same parity: a separating block has at least $\rho$ sites, while a generator has diameter at most $\rho$. The union of the even blocks is correctable because any commuting Pauli on it restricts to a commuting Pauli on each block, each necessarily a stabilizer. The odd union is likewise correctable. The cleaning lemma forbids complementary correctable regions for a nonzero code. Twisted boundary identifications only permute sites' internal coordinates, so the argument is unaffected.

Disconnected BB codes reduce to isomorphic connected components. A cover component projects with degree one or two. Degree one gives the same distance; degree two is covered by the preceding arguments. All bounds may use the length of the relevant base component, which is at most the supplied total length.

## 9. Geometric refinements: subquartic f and square-root Gamma

This section imports the published geometric theorem of Arnault, Gaborit, Rozendaal, Saussay and Zemor, *A Variant of the Bravyi-Terhal Bound for Arbitrary Boundary Conditions*, arXiv:2502.04995v2, Theorem 3.6. The earlier sections do not require that theorem.

For $s$ sites in a quotient of $\mathbf Z^D$, $m$ qubits per site and check radius $\rho$, it gives


$$
d\le m\sqrt{\gamma_D}(\sqrt D+4\rho)s^{(D-1)/D}
$$


when $s^{1/D}\ge8\rho\sqrt{\gamma_D}$. We handle smaller sizes explicitly below. We only use the safe bound $\gamma_D\le1+D/4$.

Let $n$ be the physical length of the connected base component. Its double cover has $n$ group elements. Normalizing the two trinomials gives a four-generator lattice representation, with two qubits per site and radius one. The geometric bound and the elementary small-size estimate $d_2\le n$ imply, uniformly,


$$
\boxed{d_2\le K n^{3/4},\qquad K=12\sqrt{\gamma_4}\le12\sqrt2.}
$$


Indeed, below the theorem's threshold, $n^{1/4}<8\sqrt{\gamma_4}$, and $n<8\sqrt{\gamma_4}n^{3/4}$.

### Refinement of f(d)

For a rank-zero presentation, $n\le2d^4$, so


$$
d_2\le K2^{3/4}d^3<32d^3.
$$


Rank one already gives $8d^3$. The genuinely higher-rank bound is $4d^{\log_2 9}$; the finite-check case is smaller. Hence


$$
\boxed{d_2\le\max\{32d^3,4d^{\log_2 9}\}\le32d^{\log_2 9}.}
$$


This remains superlinear, so it does not prove a finite amplification constant.

### Refinement of Gamma(N)

The rank-sensitive estimates give a better result than merely combining the quartic bound with $d_2\le Kn^{3/4}$.

At rank zero, $n\le2d^4$ implies


$$
\frac{d_2}{d}\le K2^{1/4}\sqrt n.
$$



At rank one,


$$
\frac{d_2}{d}\le\min\{8d^2,Kn^{3/4}/d\}
\le2K^{2/3}\sqrt n.
$$


The last inequality is the geometric mean with weights $1/3,2/3$.

For a genuinely rank-two presentation, quotient the cover by the full preimage of the image torsion subgroup. Let that preimage have size $h'\le2|T|$. There are $s=n/h'$ sites, $2h'$ qubits per site, and a two-dimensional quotient-lattice geometry with radius $q$. The bound $q^2|T|\le d^2$ gives


$$
q\sqrt{h'}\le\sqrt2\,d.
$$


Above the geometric theorem's threshold,


$$
\frac{d_2}{d}
\le(4+8\sqrt2)\sqrt{\gamma_2}\sqrt n.
$$


Below its threshold, $s<64q^2\gamma_2$, the elementary $d_2\le n$ gives the smaller bound $8\sqrt{2\gamma_2}\sqrt n$.

For the rank-two finite-check case, the other check's two free differences form a free basis. The same calculation uses radius one and $|T|\le d^2$.

Thus


$$
\Gamma(N)\le C\sqrt N,
$$


where


$$
C=\max\{12\,2^{1/4}\sqrt{\gamma_4},
\ 2(12\sqrt{\gamma_4})^{2/3},
\ (4+8\sqrt2)\sqrt{\gamma_2}\}.
$$


The safe bounds $\gamma_4\le2$ and $\gamma_2\le3/2$ give $C<24$. No optimality of this constant or of the exponent is claimed.

## 10. A weight-eight compact testbed, not a new distance record

The normal form includes


$$
U=\mathbf Z^2\oplus C_2,\quad h^2=1,
\qquad A=1+x+y,\quad B=1+hx^2+hy^2.
$$


With $\eta=1+h$, the weight-eight cycle


$$
z=(\eta A,\eta)
$$


generates universal $H_1$. Modulo $A$, $B$ reduces to $\eta$, whose annihilator is $(\eta)$. The canonical power generator is $(A^3,B)$, of weight twelve; it differs from $z$ by the boundary with multiplier $hA$. This is a concrete example of the weight distortion that remains after the algebraic generator problem is solved.

There are actual finite quotients where $z$ survives. Let $L\ge3$ be odd and divisible by three, take


$$
G=C_{2L}\times C_L,\qquad h=x^L,
$$


and use the unchanged-polynomial double cover to $C_{4L}\times C_L$. The checks are


$$
A=1+x+y,\qquad B=1+x^{L+2}+x^Ly^2.
$$


Both Tanner graphs are connected because $A$ contains the two coordinate generators.

For a direct nonboundary proof, map the base ring to
$\mathbf F_4[\eta]/(\eta^2)$, with $\omega^2+\omega+1=0$, by


$$
x\mapsto\omega(1+\eta),\qquad y\mapsto\omega^2.
$$


The periods hold, $h\mapsto1+\eta$, the checks become $(\omega\eta,\eta)$, and $z\mapsto(0,\eta)$. This cannot be a boundary: a vanishing first boundary component forces the second to vanish.

Upstairs, put $H=x^L$, of order four, and


$$
S=1+H+H^2+H^3.
$$


The cycle $(AS,S)$ has weight sixteen and is nontrivial. Map to $\mathbf F_4[\rho]/(\rho^4)$, taking


$$
x\mapsto\omega(1+\rho)^a,\quad y\mapsto\omega^2,
\qquad aL\equiv1\pmod4.
$$


Then $H\mapsto1+\rho$, both check images are $\rho$ times units, and the logical maps to $(0,\rho^3)$. Again, a zero first boundary component forces a zero second component.

These statements give $d\le8$ and $d_2\le16$, not exact distances. No assertion that this family has exact base distance eight is made. Finite arithmetic checks at $L=9,15,21$ verify these logicals and give dimensions $4,28,16$, respectively, on both sides. These dimensions are exact finite computations only, not a claimed formula for all $L$.

## 11. Independent verification and scope of computation

The principal theorems are symbolic and do not depend on exhaustive finite-code searches.

`verify_mechanisms.py` uses standard-library arithmetic to check:

* every minor of the twelve-root configuration needed for the determinant bounds;
* 24 nilpotent-pencil cases over the exact rational-function field $\mathbf F_2(X)$, using a separately implemented binary-polynomial fraction field;
* 176 finite-field specializations over $\mathbf F_4,\mathbf F_8,\mathbf F_{16}$, with $\alpha\ne0,1$;
* 15 negative controls at excluded coefficient values where the proposed pencil conclusion fails, confirming that the unit hypotheses are substantive;
* the explicit non-BB specialization pathology from Section 2.

`verify_finite_covers.py` checks 1,920 selected parameter combinations. In 528 cases the relevant compact base generator is nontrivial. All 528 have the promised cover witness: 245 by projection and 283 by transfer. These include 96 odd-norm cases and 312 doublings of an even coordinate. The cases allow torsion collapse and are not restricted to flat specializations.

Every finite nonboundary is accompanied by a dual separating functional. `audit_finite_certificates.py` independently reconstructs the checks and translations, verifies the cycle equations, checks that each dual functional vanishes on every boundary, and checks that it has value one on the proposed logical. It audits 1,056 primal/dual certificates and all cover term matches without importing the construction or rank routines.

`verify_weight8_testbed.py` checks the explicit weight-eight/sixteen testbed. None of these checks is a minimum-distance search, and no unsuccessful search is being used as a lower bound.

A diagnostic comparison with the supplied 684 retained six-column presentations found 16 finite-check forms, 314 Frobenius-aligned forms (290 with $q=1$, 24 with $q=2$), and 354 with no shared free triangle. This is a consistency check against supplied data, not a new classification; those 354 include the old unit and boundary cases. ## 12. Final ledger and the next precise obstruction

The newly established structure explains the earlier higher-rank collapse:

1. A genuinely two-dimensional Laurent trinomial has only Frobenius factorization.
2. Nonzero universal two-check homology therefore forces the torsion-Frobenius normal form.
3. Its two-primary part is a nilpotent pencil with a unit coefficient and a single cyclic compact homology generator.
4. The finite nontriviality of that generator is forced by any surviving compact base class, and the power-cycle argument supplies a genuinely nontrivial compact cover class.

This reason persists at arbitrary circuit weight. Eight does not introduce an unresolved algebraic compact-survival mechanism of the specified kind.

The best lower bound remains the supplied $C_*\ge7/3$. No ratio above it was established here. No finite universal numerical upper bound on $C_*$ was proved. The candidate constants $7/3,5/2,3$ remain unsettled. The new results are polynomial in base distance and square-root in truncated block length, not constant-factor bounds.

There is no unproved compact-survival lemma left in the genuinely higher-rank non-finite-check case. The unrestricted base-change assertion is false, and the report does not claim it. Low-rank and finite-check cases are controlled metrically instead of by a general specialization-injectivity assertion.

**Single next target: linear compact replacement in the torsion-Frobenius normal form.** For


$$
A=1+X+Y,\qquad B=1+hX^{2^s}+kY^{2^s},
$$


fix a compatible finite quotient and a universal cover. Define


$$
\delta_{\rm comp}=
\min\{|z|:z\text{ is a universal base cycle whose finite image is nontrivial}\},
$$


and define $\widetilde\delta_{\rm comp}$ similarly using universal cover cycles. The new theorem proves finiteness and a polynomial control, but not


$$
\widetilde\delta_{\rm comp}\le3\delta_{\rm comp}.
$$


Prove or refute that explicit physical-weight inequality, allowing boundary adjustment and all compact cover classes, not only the initially transferred class. In a minimum-circuit presentation, $\delta_{\rm comp}=d$. A proof would settle the factor-three issue in the genuinely higher-rank regime; low-rank linear bounds would still require separate work. A failure would identify genuine metric distortion after the universal survival obstruction has been removed, but would not by itself certify a violation of the finite cover-distance inequality.
