# Polynomial protection against strict-trinomial BB cover degradation

**Status: IMPROVED_TRINOMIAL_DEGRADATION_BOUNDS.**

Date: 6 September 2026.

This note gives a symbolic argument. Its theorems do not depend on the earlier weight-six or weight-eight computer classifications. Small exact computations accompanying the note check the new witness formulas and several arithmetic identities; they are not used to extrapolate a classification.

## 1. Main results and their exact scope

Let pi: G_tilde -> G be a surjection of finite abelian groups whose kernel is a 2-group. Let A_tilde and B_tilde be binary group-algebra polynomials with exactly three distinct supported monomials each, and suppose their images A and B also have exactly three distinct supported monomials each. Use the BB complex



$$
R\xrightarrow{\partial_2}R^2\xrightarrow{\partial_1}R,
\qquad \partial_2s=(Bs,As),\quad \partial_1(u,v)=Au+Bv,
\quad R=\mathbb F_2[G].
$$



Assume the base has positive dimension. Write d for its distance, n=2|G| for its length, and w for the cover distance.

**Theorem A.**



$$
\boxed{d\le 4w^3.}
\tag{1}
$$



In particular every valid strict-trinomial binary BB double cover obeys



$$
\boxed{d_2\ge(d/4)^{1/3}.}
\tag{2}
$$



Consequently there is a universal finite D_w for every fixed possible cover distance w, and one may take D_w=4w^3. More generally, a sequence with bounded cover distances has bounded base distances. This closes the entire bounded-cover-distance route, not merely the distance-ten case. The earlier constants D_6=56 and D_8=96 remain much sharper at their particular weights.

**Theorem B (ambient estimate).** Every positive-dimensional strict-trinomial binary abelian BB code satisfies



$$
\boxed{
 d\le C_0 n^{3/4},\qquad
 C_0=\frac{16}{3}\left(\frac{12}{\pi^2}\right)^{3/4}.
}
\tag{3}
$$



This is an explicit consequence of a strip/cleaning bound and Minkowski's second theorem, proved below. It is not claimed that the exponent in this standalone ambient estimate is new in the coding literature.

Combining (1), (3), and d<=n/2 gives



$$
\boxed{
\gamma_-^{(3)}(N)\ge
\max\left\{N^{-2/3},\frac{c_0}{\sqrt N}\right\},
\quad
c_0=\frac{\pi\,3^{1/6}}{2^{13/3}}
=0.187157298274\ldots .
}
\tag{4}
$$



The bound applies whenever the defining collection is nonempty. The inherited small-weight results additionally give



$$
\gamma_-^{(3)}(N)\ge
\max\left\{
\min\{1/12,20/N\},\ N^{-2/3},\ c_0N^{-1/2}
\right\}.
\tag{5}
$$



Only the first entry of (5) uses the preceding weight-six/eight classifications. The new asymptotic conclusion is



$$
\gamma_-^{(3)}(N)=\Omega(N^{-1/2}),\qquad
\Delta^{(3)}(N)=O(\sqrt N).
$$



No positive universal constant and no vanishing-ratio construction are proved here. The supplied exact 14-to-12 construction, and hence the retained upper bound 6/7 for N>=636, are inherited inputs, not newly distance-certified by this note.

The BB covering convention is the termwise group projection of Symons, Rajput and Browne [1]. The restriction to a 2-group kernel is important in one spectral-descent step below. We do not claim Theorem A for arbitrary odd-degree quotients.

## 2. Preliminary reductions

### 2.1 Weight, homology and normalization

Group inversion followed by block exchange identifies the two CSS homologies without changing weight. Thus the distance can be computed in the displayed complex. It also identifies the two check ranks and gives



$$
k=2\dim_{\mathbb F_2}R/(A,B).
\tag{6}
$$



Multiplying A or B by a monomial gives a weight-preserving chain isomorphism. Normalize both polynomials to have a constant term:



$$
A=1+a_1+a_2,\qquad B=1+b_1+b_2.
\tag{7}
$$



The normalization is done compatibly upstairs and downstairs.

Let K be the subgroup generated by these four term differences. The complex over G is a direct sum of identical complexes on K-cosets. Its distance is the distance of the K-code, and its positive dimension is equivalent to positive dimension of that code. Thus nonconnected Tanner graphs cause no difficulty. The normalized support subgroup upstairs maps onto the normalized support subgroup downstairs, with kernel still a 2-group.

A minimum nonboundary cycle has circuit support. Otherwise a nonzero proper subcycle and its complement are both smaller cycles, at least one of which is a nonboundary. This contradicts minimality.

### 2.2 A finite-check subgroup bound

**Lemma 2.1.** If one normalized check polynomial is supported on a finite subgroup K of a finite abelian group, then positive dimension implies d<=|K|.

Indeed R is free over F_2[K]. Therefore ker A is generated as an R-module by elements supported on K, each of weight at most |K|. Positive dimension implies ker A intersect ker B is nonzero: transpose the nonsurjective matrix [A B] and apply group inversion. Hence B acts singularly, and therefore nonsurjectively, on ker A. Its image is an R-submodule. Some one of the indicated generators u is outside B(ker A). Then (u,0) is a nonboundary cycle of weight at most |K|.

In particular, taking K=G gives d<=|G|=n/2.

### 2.3 A strip bound and its cofactor formulation

**Lemma 2.2.** Suppose the normalized check translations factor through a finitely generated abelian group H=T direct_sum Z. Let R_0 be the larger of the two check-support spans in a primitive free coordinate. Every positive-dimensional finite image satisfies



$$
d\le4|T|R_0.
\tag{8}
$$



To prove this, quotient the finite image by the image of T. Its cells form a cyclic group of order P, possibly with a torsion twist at the cyclic seam, and each cell contains at most 2|T| qubits.

Both stabilizer types can simultaneously be made to have cell span at most R_0. If the A support runs from a_min to a_max and the B support from b_min to b_max, shift left qubits by -b_min and right qubits by -a_min. The two parts of each Z check then have a common lower endpoint, while the two parts of each X check have a common upper endpoint.

For P<2R_0 the ambient bound already suffices. Otherwise partition the circle into an even number of alternating intervals, each having between R_0 and 2R_0 cells. Such a partition exists by starting with 2 floor(P/(2R_0)) intervals of length R_0 and distributing the remaining fewer than 2R_0 cells.

If d>4|T|R_0, every interval is correctable. No generator meets two intervals of the same colour: an intervening interval of R_0 cells separates their nearest sites by R_0+1. Any commuting Pauli supported on a same-colour union therefore has separately commuting restrictions to its intervals. Each restriction is a stabilizer, so the union is correctable. Both complementary colours would then be correctable, contradicting cleaning and k>0. This proves (8).

Here cleaning is the usual finite symplectic-linear-algebra statement. If a logical's restriction to a correctable region could not be cancelled by a stabilizer restriction, a separating linear functional would give a Pauli supported in that region which commutes with every stabilizer but anticommutes with that logical, contradicting correctability.

In particular, take three independent relation rows r_1,r_2,r_3 in Z^4 and let c be their integer cofactor vector. If tau is the gcd of the coordinates of c, then



$$
\mathbb Z^4/\langle r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle=T\oplus\mathbb Z,
\qquad |T|=\tau,
$$



and c/tau is its primitive free coordinate. Every positive-dimensional finite quotient of this group satisfies



$$
\boxed{
d\le4\max\{\operatorname{span}(0,c_1,c_2),
                 \operatorname{span}(0,c_3,c_4)\}.
}
\tag{9}
$$



The three rows need not generate all actual exponent relations. Additional relations merely give another finite quotient to which the same bound applies.

## 3. Presentations from a circuit of arbitrary weight

Take a minimum cover logical Y of weight w. Pair all syndrome incidences, including arbitrary pairings at sites with multiplicity four or higher. There are 3w incidences. The pairing graph is connected and cubic on w vertices. It has no loops because the check terms are distinct. Parallel edges, including the weight-two triple-edge graph, are allowed here.

Label each vertex by its qubit block and its three ports by the three terms of the corresponding normalized check. For an edge joining ports i,j at vertices u,v impose



$$
p_u+c_{u,i}=p_v+c_{v,j}.
\tag{10}
$$



A spanning tree determines all relative qubit positions. Each non-tree edge supplies one integral relation on the four check translations. Write



$$
H=\mathbb Z^4/L
\tag{11}
$$



for the resulting mixed-block presentation. Both the actual cover support subgroup and the base support subgroup are quotients of H. The selected circuit is a genuine finite-support cycle over F_2[H]. Its qubits remain distinct there because a collision in H would also be a collision in the actual cover.

Each fundamental graph cycle has length at most w. Traversing it writes its relation row as a sum of at most w members of



$$
\mathcal D=
\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2,\pm(e_1-e_2),
  \pm e_3,\pm e_4,\pm(e_3-e_4)\}.
\tag{12}
$$



Indeed, at each traversed vertex one subtracts the labels of its two distinct traversed ports. Thus every relation row has Euclidean norm at most sqrt(2)w.

Every determinant of four members of D is 0, 1 or -1. A nonzero determinant requires two vectors in each coordinate block, and any two planar vectors from {e_1,e_2,e_1-e_2} have determinant 0 or +/-1.

Suppose rank L>=3 and choose three independent fundamental relation rows. For their cofactor vector c and any delta in D,



$$
|c\cdot\delta|
=|\det(r_1,r_2,r_3,\delta)|\le w^3.
\tag{13}
$$



This follows by multilinearity and (12): the expansion contains at most w^3 determinants of root vectors, each of magnitude at most one. The maximum on the left is exactly the maximum span in (9). Consequently



$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}L\ge3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\le4w^3.}
\tag{14}
$$



This single argument handles both finite and virtually cyclic presentations, at every circuit weight.

For a pure-block circuit use only the two translations of its check. A strict trinomial is a nonzerodivisor in its generated group algebra whenever that group has positive free rank. To see this, select a free-coordinate direction where one supported term is uniquely extremal; when two free positions coincide choose the remaining position as the extremum. The extremal coefficient is a monomial and hence a unit. Multiplication is injective by comparing extremal degrees.

Thus a pure universal cycle forces its two-generator presentation to be finite. Two independent fundamental relation rows give, by Hadamard,



$$
|H_A|\le2w^2.
$$



Lemma 2.1 therefore gives



$$
\boxed{\text{pure circuit}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad d\le2w^2.}
\tag{15}
$$



It remains to analyze mixed presentations of free rank at least two. No enumeration of graphs or ports will be needed.

## 4. The higher-free-rank structure theorem

Write H=T direct_sum Z^r, with T finite. Split T into its odd-order part and its 2-primary part. After extending scalars to an algebraic closure of F_2, split the odd-order group algebra into character components. In any such component, reduction modulo the augmentation ideal of the 2-primary part gives a Laurent polynomial ring over a field.

### 4.1 Coprime reductions force universal boundaries

**Lemma 4.1.** In a character component, if the reduced checks A_bar,B_bar are nonzero and coprime, every universal cycle is a boundary in that component.

Over the Laurent polynomial domain, Au+Bv=0 and gcd(A,B)=1 imply u=Bs and v=As. Lift s and subtract the corresponding boundary. The remainder lies in the nilpotent augmentation ideal. Repeat on its successive powers. Each graded layer is a free module over the Laurent polynomial ring, with the same reduced differential. Nilpotence terminates the process and gives a boundary in the original component.

This is also the elementary filtered version of Koszul exactness for a regular pair [3]. No assumption is made that a formal cycle which passes a local shortening test is nontrivial.

### 4.2 Newton polygons eliminate all free ranks at least three

A trinomial whose free support is not a point never reduces to zero in a character component. If its free support has two points, the singleton point has a nonzero coefficient. If it has three points, each coefficient is nonzero.

The Newton polytope of a product is the Minkowski sum of those of its factors [2]. A segment cannot be a nontrivial Minkowski summand of a nondegenerate triangle. Every non-point Minkowski summand of a triangle is itself a positively homothetic triangle: its edge directions must be among the triangle's three directions, and the unique positive closing relation fixes their relative lengths.

Therefore a common factor of two nondegenerate triangular trinomials forces their free support triangles to be positively homothetic and to lie in the same plane. A polynomial with segment Newton polytope cannot share a nonunit factor with a triangular trinomial; two nonparallel segment supports are likewise coprime.

If one check has point free support, it is supported on T and Lemma 2.1 already bounds the distance. Otherwise these observations show that all character components are regular whenever the combined free rank is at least three. Hence



$$
\boxed{r\ge3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_1(A,B;\mathbb F_2[H])=0.}
\tag{16}
$$



A minimum cover logical cannot have such a universal presentation.

### 4.3 Rank two leaves only Frobenius-related triangles

Assume r=2. A finite-supported check gives d<=|T|. If neither support is finite and a component is nonregular, both free supports must be triangles and be positively homothetic.

After choosing corresponding reference vertices and permuting terms, write the free exponents as



$$
A:\ 0,pu,pv,\qquad B:\ 0,qu,qv,
\tag{17}
$$



where p,q are positive coprime integers. The vectors u,v are integral: a Bezout combination of the corresponding A and B exponents produces each of them. They generate the whole free group because the four check differences do. Hence u,v are a lattice basis.

In a character component the two polynomials therefore have forms



$$
1+\alpha X^p+\beta Y^p,
\qquad
1+\gamma X^q+\delta Y^q.
$$



Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic two, writing p=2^a p_0 with p_0 odd expresses the first polynomial as a 2^a-th power of a scalar Fermat polynomial of degree p_0. That latter polynomial is absolutely irreducible. Its projective homogenization is smooth, since its three partial derivatives cannot vanish simultaneously; a reducible projective plane curve would have intersecting components and a singular point. The case p_0=1 is immediate. See [4] for the standard plane-curve facts.

A common factor thus forces the odd parts of p and q to agree. Their coprimality forces both odd parts to be one. Interchanging checks when needed, p=1 and q=2^s.

Lift the smaller triangle's two basis exponents to elements x,y of H. They split off its free part. Consequently



$$
\boxed{
H=T\oplus\langle x,y\rangle,\quad
A=1+x+y,\quad B=1+t x^q+u y^q,
\quad q=2^s,\quad T=\langle t,u\rangle.
}
\tag{18}
$$



The torsion subgroup has at most two generators. In this splitting A has coefficient one on all three terms. The reduced checks in an odd character chi have a common factor exactly when



$$
\chi(t)=\chi(u)=1.
$$



Thus the only potentially nonregular odd character is the trivial one on all of T_odd.

This classifies the possible higher-free-rank mechanism at every circuit weight. It does not say that every finite realization of (18) has a short logical: the relevant odd-character summand must actually encode. That condition is essential and is checked in Section 6.

## 5. A two-generator nilpotent-twist lemma

The following lemma controls the entire Frobenius mechanism, including arbitrary 2-primary torsion orders and arbitrary Frobenius powers.

**Lemma 5.1.** In a finite abelian group algebra let



$$
A=1+x+y,\qquad B=1+t x^q+u y^q,\qquad q=2^s,
$$



and put T_0=<t,u>. Let O be its odd-order subgroup and



$$
E=\sum_{o\in O}o.
$$



Suppose the E-summand has positive dimension, equivalently E R/(A,B) is nonzero. Then



$$
\boxed{d\le|T_0|(1+3^s).}
\tag{19}
$$



When T_0 is a 2-group, E=1 and positive dimension of the whole code suffices.

### 5.1 Constructing a globally defined short cycle

Write the 2-primary part of T_0 as



$$
K=\langle g\rangle\times\langle h\rangle
\cong C_M\times C_N,
\qquad N\mid M,
$$



where M,N are powers of two and N=1 is allowed. Put e=1+g, f=1+h. In the E-summand the odd parts of t,u act as one. Write their 2-primary parts as g^a h^b and g^c h^d. They generate K, so when M>1 at least one of a,c is odd.

Set C=B+A^q. In the E-summand it has the form



$$
C=eF+fH,
\qquad
\overline F=(a\bmod2)x^q+(c\bmod2)y^q
\tag{20}
$$



modulo (e,f). At any common maximal ideal of A,B in the E-summand, F is a unit: its residue is x^q, y^q, or x^q+y^q=1.

Define the binary polynomial



$$
S=E\,e^{M-N}C^{N-1}.
\tag{21}
$$



Since N is a power of two,



$$
EC^N=Ee^NF^N,
\qquad CS=0.
$$



Consequently



$$
\boxed{W=(SA^{q-1},S)}
\tag{22}
$$



is a cycle in the actual finite code.

For M=N=1, interpret the zero exponents as usual, so S=E and EC=0.

### 5.2 The local annihilator identities

We prove nontriviality, rather than inferring it from the existence of the cycle.

Localize at a common maximal ideal in the E-summand. The group algebra is free over F_2[K]. For I=(e,f), its associated graded algebra is



$$
(R/I)[e,f]/(e^M,f^N).
\tag{23}
$$



This remains true after localization and the odd-character projection: group coset representatives give a free basis, and the torsion carry in their products is one in degree zero.

The initial form of C is e F_bar+f H_bar, with F_bar a unit. Substitution



$$
e\longmapsto e\overline F+f\overline H,
\qquad f\longmapsto f
$$



is an automorphism of (23). Both it and its inverse preserve the truncation relations, since f^M=0 follows from N|M. Thus the initial form is an exact length-M nilpotent parameter.

Lifting through the finite I-filtration gives



$$
\operatorname{Ann}(C)=C^{M-1}R,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ann}(C^{M-1})=CR.
\tag{24}
$$



For clarity, if Cr=0, the lowest nonzero graded part of r is divisible by the (M-1)-st power of the initial form of C. Subtract a corresponding C^{M-1} multiple and increase the filtration degree. Iteration terminates and proves the first identity. The same argument with C^{M-1} proves the second.

Also



$$
C^{M-1}=F^{M-N}e^{M-N}C^{N-1},
$$



so S, whose E factor is one locally, is a unit multiple of C^{M-1}. In particular



$$
\operatorname{Ann}(C)=SR,
\qquad \operatorname{Ann}(S)=CR.
\tag{25}
$$



### 5.3 Actual nonboundary certificate and weight bound

If W were a boundary, there would be r with



$$
Ar=S,\qquad Br=SA^{q-1}.
$$



These equations give Cr=0. By (25), r=Sv. Then S(Av+1)=0, so Av+1 belongs to CR. This is impossible in the chosen local ring: A,C are in its maximal ideal but Av+1 is a unit. Thus W is a genuine nonboundary.

For the weight bound, e^{M-N} has exactly M/N supported monomials. The polynomial C=B+A^q has at most four supported monomials. Frobenius therefore gives



$$
|C^{N-1}|\le4^{\log_2N}=N^2,
\qquad |A^{q-1}|\le3^s.
$$



It follows that



$$
|S|\le|O|\frac{M}{N}N^2=|O|MN=|T_0|,
$$



and |W|<=|T_0|(1+3^s), proving Lemma 5.1.

The shortening from a possible M^2 bound to MN is important. It uses S=e^{M-N}C^{N-1}, rather than simply taking C^{M-1}.

## 6. Descending the active character and bounding rank-two torsion

Return to a mixed circuit presentation H of free rank two.

If one check is torsion-supported, two independent fundamental relation rows bound



$$
|T|\le2w^2
$$



by the gcd-of-minors formula and Hadamard. Lemma 2.1 applies downstairs.

Otherwise Lemma 4.1 and Section 4.3 show that a nonboundary cover realization requires the Frobenius form (18), and that its universal homology is supported only in the trivial T_odd character. Let E be the corresponding odd norm. The other character components of the universal cycle are boundaries, and remain boundaries after passage to the cover. Thus



$$
[Y]=[EY]\ne0
$$



in cover homology. There is a common maximal ideal of the cover checks where E is one.

The kernel of F_2[G_tilde] -> F_2[G] is nilpotent. Indeed it is generated by 1+z for elements z of the finite abelian 2-group kernel; these generators are nilpotent and commute. Hence projection gives a bijection of maximal ideals. The common maximal ideal just selected descends to a base common maximal ideal where the projected odd norm is still one.

The E-summand therefore encodes downstairs. This proves exactly the hypothesis needed for Lemma 5.1; it does not assume that the particular projected cover logical survives.

Finally, in the normalized coordinates of (18), every relation row has form



$$
(-q\beta_1,-q\beta_2,\beta_1,\beta_2).
\tag{26}
$$



The saturated relation lattice is the graph of -q times the identity. Projecting to the last two coordinates identifies the relation lattice with a rank-two sublattice L_beta of Z^2, and



$$
|T|=[\mathbb Z^2:L_\beta].
$$



Choose two independent fundamental relation rows. Their Euclidean norms are at most sqrt(2)w, so their beta-vector norms are at most sqrt(2)w/sqrt(q^2+1). Thus



$$
|T|\le\frac{2w^2}{q^2+1}.
\tag{27}
$$



The term renormalizations needed to obtain (18) do not invalidate the row bound: each fundamental cycle still has at most w vertices and each vertex still contributes a difference of two normalized ports.

If T_0 is the actual torsion image in the base, |T_0|<=|T|. By Lemma 5.1 and q=2^s,



$$
d\le|T_0|(1+3^s)
 \le\frac{2w^2(1+3^s)}{1+4^s}
 \le2w^2.
\tag{28}
$$



Thus every possible mixed presentation of free rank two also forces a quadratic base bound.

### Why the active-summand hypothesis cannot be dropped

The earlier unbounded-filling family provides a useful adversarial check. Let L=4^j, t=x^L in the ring with x^{3L}=y^{3L}=1, and use



$$
A=1+x+y,\qquad B=1+tx+t^2y.
$$



Here T=<t>=C_3 and q=1. Nevertheless the preceding proof state gives d>=L, so a claim d<=2|T|=6 based only on global k>0 would be false.

There is no contradiction with Lemma 5.1. For E=1+t+t^2,



$$
A^L E=y^L E,
$$



so A is a unit in the E-summand. That summand is acyclic, and (E,E) is a boundary. The missing hypothesis would have been precisely the hypothesis supplied by the surviving universal cover cycle and the nilpotent projection in the proof above.

This also explains why a large coefficient filling in that family cannot simply be converted into a fixed-weight logical by a binary cover.

## 7. Proof of the cubic theorem

Choose a minimum cover logical of weight w and its connected incidence presentation.

A pure circuit gives d<=2w^2 by (15). For a mixed circuit, relation rank at least three gives d<=4w^3 by (14). Relation rank at most one means free rank at least three, where every universal cycle is a boundary by (16), so this case cannot occur. The remaining relation-rank-two cases give d<=2w^2 by (28) or the finite-check lemma.

Since w>=2, every case satisfies d<=4w^3. This proves Theorem A without a finite graph classification.

The proof controls an actual base nonboundary or uses the distance bound for the whole base code. It never identifies the distance with that of a distinguished transferred class and never assumes that subtracting a lifted boundary preserves weight.

## 8. The ambient bound and the N^{-1/2} degradation scale

Normalize the checks and restrict to their generated subgroup K, of order M. The full lattice of relations



$$
\Gamma=\ker(\mathbb Z^4\to K)
$$



has determinant M.

Use the positive definite metric



$$
Q=\begin{pmatrix}
1&1/2&0&0\\
1/2&1&0&0\\
0&0&1&1/2\\
0&0&1/2&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det Q=9/16.
\tag{29}
$$



Every port difference in D has Q-norm one. If r_1,r_2,r_3 are independent relation rows with cofactor vector c, then



$$
\max_{\delta\in\mathcal D}|c\cdot\delta|
\le\sqrt{c^TQ^{-1}c}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det Q}}\sqrt{\det(r_iQr_j^T)_{i,j=1}^3}
\le\frac43\prod_{i=1}^3\|r_i\|_Q.
$$



Equation (9) consequently gives



$$
d\le\frac{16}{3}\prod_{i=1}^3\|r_i\|_Q.
\tag{30}
$$



Let lambda_1<=...<=lambda_4 be the successive minima of Gamma in this norm. The Q-unit ball has volume 2pi^2/3. Minkowski's second theorem [5] gives



$$
\prod_{i=1}^4\lambda_i\le\frac{24M}{\pi^2}.
$$



Choose independent lattice vectors attaining the first three minima. Since lambda_4^3>=lambda_1lambda_2lambda_3,



$$
\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3
\le\left(\prod_{i=1}^4\lambda_i\right)^{3/4}.
$$



Combining with (30), and using 2M<=n, proves



$$
d\le\frac{16}{3}\left(\frac{24M}{\pi^2}\right)^{3/4}
\le\frac{16}{3}\left(\frac{12n}{\pi^2}\right)^{3/4},
$$



which is Theorem B.

From d<=4w^3,



$$
\frac wd\ge4^{-1/3}d^{-2/3}.
$$



Using d<=n/2 gives w/d>=n^{-2/3}. Using Theorem B gives



$$
\frac wd\ge
\left(4C_0^2\right)^{-1/3}n^{-1/2}
=\frac{\pi3^{1/6}}{2^{13/3}}n^{-1/2}.
$$



Taking infima over n<=N proves (4). This is a lower bound, not an assertion that the infimum has this order.

## 9. Executed checks, inherited inputs and limitations

The uniform proofs above are symbolic. The following exact computations are supplied separately.

* `verify_twists.py` checks 758 finite twisted-Frobenius instances by exact binary elimination. It verifies strict support counts, zero syndrome, positive dimension, nonboundary status and the stated weight bound. The tests include cyclic and noncyclic 2-primary subgroups, invariant factors up to (8,8) and (16,2), Frobenius powers 1,2,4,8, odd norm orders 1,3,5, and codes with up to 1,152 qubits. No minimum distances are computed.
* `verify_geometry.py` independently evaluates all 20,736 ordered quadruples of the twelve port differences. It also checks 200 exact Gram/cofactor identities and 256 exact Fermat-polynomial gcds. The proofs of those identities and the unrestricted factor classification are given above; the tests are not their logical basis.
* `verify_odd_sector.py` checks the explicit acyclic odd-norm sector in the earlier filling family, including its weight-six boundary and uniform inverse identity. This is a guard against the invalid omission of the active-summand hypothesis.

The program outputs are `twist_verification.json`, `geometry_verification.json`, and `odd_sector_verification.json`. The proof is not a proof-assistant formalization or an external referee report.

The inherited D_6=56, D_8=96 and 6/7 construction are not dependencies of Theorems A or B. The first two are used only to retain the sharper small-size entry in (5). The exact 6/7 distance claim remains at its supplied certification status; this work does not newly certify its base or cover minimum distances.

The two-check filling lemma remains false, and the previously established weight-six filling distortion remains unbounded. These are not contradicted by the new theorem: a small base boundary with a large filling is not itself a small cover logical.

The new conclusion changes the fixed-weight frontier fundamentally. There is now no bounded value of d_2 compatible with d tending to infinity. Any possible vanishing-ratio family must have d_2 tending to infinity, and must respect d_2>=(d/4)^{1/3}. No such family is constructed here.

## 10. A precise next obstruction

The quadratic bounds in Sections 3 and 6 already handle pure circuits and every surviving presentation with free rank at least two. The only cubic loss is the cofactor/strip estimate for mixed circuits whose relation lattice has rank at least three.

**Next lemma to prove or refute (quadratic circuit-strip descent).** There is an absolute C such that, whenever a weight-w minimum cover logical has a mixed incidence presentation H=Z^4/L with rank L>=3 and has an admissible finite 2-group-kernel quotient onto a positive-dimensional strict-trinomial base, that base satisfies d<=Cw^2.

This is the sole missing case for a universal quadratic replacement of Theorem A. Its proof would improve the full degradation lower bound to Omega(N^{-3/8}) using Theorem B. Its failure would identify the low-rank finite/strip mechanism that obstructs that improvement. The universal positive-constant question would still require further work beyond a quadratic law.
