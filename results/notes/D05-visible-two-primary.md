# Two-bit local homology and the visible-2-torsion obstruction

Date: 2026-09-06.

**Principal status: TRINOMIAL_STILL_OPEN.** A universal quadratic distance bound is neither proved nor refuted here. The new results classify the *actual finite local homology modules and double-cover maps* for strict-trinomial BB codes. They do not give a weight-preserving normal form, and they do not identify the compact image of an infinite incidence presentation with all finite homology.

## 1. Scope and statements

The physical complex is



$$
\mathcal C(A,B):\quad R\xrightarrow{r\mapsto(Br,Ar)}R^2
\xrightarrow{(u,v)\mapsto Au+Bv}R,\qquad R=\mathbb F_2[G].
$$



Here G is a finite abelian group generated by at most two elements, as for a rectangular BB code. Each check has three distinct monomials. Normalize each check by a monomial so its constant term is 1, and restrict to the subgroup generated by the four term differences. The complex splits into identical coset components, so this restriction preserves distance. Its Sylow-2 subgroup has at most two cyclic factors.

For a double cover, the corresponding support-subgroup map either has trivial kernel, in which case the component codes are isomorphic, or has kernel of order two. The latter case is considered below. The three supported monomials of each check must match individually under projection and remain distinct in both actual codes. These are the conventions of Symons, Rajput, and Browne, *Sequences of Bivariate Bicycle Codes from Covering Graphs*, arXiv:2511.13560v3, Theorem 3.1.

The rank-at-most-two hypothesis is about the **actual finite support group**, not the four-generated universal incidence presentation. The results below do not claim a classification of arbitrary four-generated finite abelian codes or of their infinite compact homology.

Extend scalars to a finite splitting field K for the odd-order characters. Each active local factor has residue field K. Field extension, Fourier decomposition, and the subsequent changes of coordinates are used algebraically, not as Hamming isometries.

Write



$$
N_m=K[t]/(t^m),\qquad N_0=0.
$$



### Theorem 1: two-bit local modules

At each active local factor of a support-generated strict-trinomial BB code,



$$
H_0\cong N_m,\qquad H_1\cong N_m\oplus N_m,\qquad H_2\cong N_m.
\tag{1.1}
$$



Moreover



$$
\boxed{m=2^a\quad\text{or}\quad m=2^a+2^b\ (a>b\ge0).}
\tag{1.2}
$$



In particular, m has at most two nonzero binary digits. If the Sylow-2 subgroup is cyclic, m=1. If it is C_M x C_N, with M,N powers of two, then m<=min(M,N).

These are module isomorphisms over the common quotient algebra H_0, with its induced group-algebra action. They are not statements about the minimum weights of the module generators.

### Theorem 2: double-cover normal form

For the corresponding active factors of a nontrivial double cover, let the base length be m and the cover length be m_+. Set rho=m_+-m. Then



$$
0\le\rho\le m.
$$



If rho>0, both m and rho are powers of two. There are compatible choices of the two module generators in (1.1) for which



$$
\boxed{
 p_*:N_{m+\rho}^2\longrightarrow N_m^2,
 \qquad (u,v)\longmapsto
 (u\bmod t^m,\ t^{m-\rho}v\bmod t^m),
}
\tag{1.3}
$$



and



$$
\boxed{
 \tau_*:N_m^2\longrightarrow N_{m+\rho}^2,
 \qquad (u,v)\longmapsto(t^m u,\ t^\rho v).
}
\tag{1.4}
$$



When rho=0, these read p_*(u,v)=(u,0), tau_*(u,v)=(0,v).

Thus the possible forms are:

* stable length: rho=0;
* full doubling: rho=m, with m a power of two;
* partial doubling: m=2^a and rho=2^b with b<a.

In these coordinates



$$
\ker p_*=(t^mN_{m+\rho})\oplus(t^\rho N_{m+\rho}),
\tag{1.5}
$$



and both kernel and image have abstract module type



$$
N_\rho\oplus N_m.
\tag{1.6}
$$



The cokernel has type N_{m-rho}. All these statements concern actual finite homology. No assertion is made that either displayed summand is canonically the compact summand of a chosen universal presentation.

## 2. A check is smooth at every active point

In an odd-character component, the local group algebra is



$$
K[e,f]/(e^M,f^N),
$$



with M,N powers of two; N=1 accommodates a cyclic Sylow subgroup. Temporarily regard the checks as formal series in the ambient regular ring P=K[[e,f]].

At a common zero, a normalized check is



$$
A=1+\alpha U+\beta V,\qquad \alpha+\beta=1,
$$



where U,V are group monomials with constant term 1, and alpha,beta are nonzero. Let v_1,v_2 be their parity vectors in the relevant Sylow coordinates. The linear part is



$$
\alpha v_1+\beta v_2.
\tag{2.1}
$$



This vector vanishes precisely when v_1=v_2=0. To verify this, equal nonzero vectors give their sum alpha+beta=1 times that vector; distinct nonzero binary vectors are linearly independent over K; the remaining cases contain a single nonzero term.

Since the four normalized supports generate the Sylow subgroup, at least one check has a nonzero linear part. Exchange A,B if necessary so A is smooth. The formal implicit-function theorem then gives



$$
P/(A)\cong D:=K[[t]].
\tag{2.2}
$$



If the Sylow subgroup is cyclic, at least one check has a nonzero derivative in its only group coordinate. On quotienting that check and the other (linear) ambient period, the result is K. Thus m=1 in the cyclic case.

There is a useful strengthening when the Sylow rank is two. A check whose two parity vectors span one binary line has linear part on that line. A check whose parity vectors span the plane has linear part on no binary line: in a binary basis its two coefficients are alpha,beta, both nonzero with alpha+beta=1, so they cannot be equal. Therefore a rank-one check and a rank-two check have independent gradients. Two rank-one checks must have distinct lines if together they generate the Sylow plane, and again have independent gradients.

Consequently, if the common quotient has length m>1, at least one check has two term differences that form a basis modulo 2. Choose that check as A.

## 3. Why the finite homology is exactly two copies of one truncated DVR

Write the actual local coefficient ring as



$$
R=P/(F,G),
$$



where F,G are the two independent period equations. They form a regular sequence in P. Put



$$
b=B\bmod A,\quad f=F\bmod A,\quad g=G\bmod A
$$



in D=K[[t]]. Resolving R by the Koszul complex on F,G, and then resolving P/(A) by the regular element A, gives



$$
H_i\bigl(K(A,B;R)\bigr)
\cong H_i\bigl(K(b,f,g;D)\bigr).
\tag{3.1}
$$



This is an ordinary comparison of the total Koszul complex K(A,B,F,G;P) in two orders. No flat-specialization assertion for H_1 is used.

At least one of b,f,g is nonzero, because independent finite periods cannot both vanish identically on the smooth A-curve. In the DVR D, their ideal is (t^m). An invertible change of the three Koszul generators changes the sequence to



$$
(t^m,0,0).
$$



Since t^m is a nonzerodivisor on D, its Koszul homology is N_m in degree zero and zero in degree one. Tensoring with the exterior complex on two zero generators gives



$$
H_0=N_m,\quad H_1=N_m^2,\quad H_2=N_m,\quad H_3=0.
$$



This proves (1.1). In particular, neither a second independent nilpotent parameter in H_0 nor unequal Jordan lengths in its two H_1 copies survives at an actual active finite component.

The standard facts about regular Koszul sequences used here are recorded in the Stacks Project, Section 15.31, tag 062D. The comparison and the module computation above supply the special argument needed here.

## 4. Root multiplicities of three S-units

The arithmetic restriction (1.2) follows from the following elementary local statement. It applies both to integer exponents and to the 2-adic exponents arising from an odd-determinant change of monomial coordinates.

### Lemma 3: two-bit contact

Let K be perfect of characteristic two and alpha not equal to 0 or 1. Consider three nonzero monomial functions in X and 1+X, with coefficients in K, near X=alpha. Integer exponents may have either sign. More generally, allow 2-adic exponents on the normalized units X/alpha and (1+X)/(1+alpha), defined by their formal binomial expansions.

If their sum F is not identically zero and vanishes at alpha, its order m has at most two nonzero binary digits.

#### Binomial preliminary

A nonzero binomial of such units, vanishing at alpha, has order a power of two. Divide by one unit. If the exponent difference is (a,b), factor out 2^s where s=min(v_2(a),v_2(b)). After taking this Frobenius power out, the logarithmic derivative at alpha is



$$
\frac{\bar a}{\alpha}+\frac{\bar b}{1+\alpha},
$$



where bar a,bar b are binary and not both zero. It never vanishes for alpha not 0,1. The binomial therefore has exact order 2^s. If the exponent difference is zero, the binomial is either a unit or identically zero, which is handled separately.

#### Remove a common Frobenius power

Divide F by one of its monomials so one term is 1. Extract the largest common 2^s from the four exponent differences. Perfectness supplies the coefficient roots. It suffices to classify the resulting primitive sum, then multiply its order by 2^s.

Its three parity positions cannot all coincide.

If there are two parity positions, choose the uniquely positioned term as the constant term by dividing by it. The other terms can be written M P^2 and M Q^2, with M having nonzero parity. Thus



$$
F=1+M(P+Q)^2.
$$



At a root, P+Q is nonzero, while M' is nonzero there by the binomial preliminary. Hence F'=M'(P+Q)^2 is nonzero: the root is simple.

If there are three parity positions, a permutation of the punctures 0,1,infinity and a monomial normalization send them to 0,(1,0),(0,1). The six substitutions generated by X -> 1+X and X -> 1/X induce GL(2,F_2) on the two parity coordinates; they are unramified away from the punctures. The resulting sum has the form



$$
F=P^2+XQ^2+(1+X)R^2
  =(P+R)^2+X(Q+R)^2.
\tag{4.1}
$$



Set U=P+R and V=Q+R. A repeated root of F has U(alpha)=V(alpha)=0, since F'=V^2. Each finite positive order of U,V is a power of two by the binomial preliminary.

If the orders differ, the lower one dominates (4.1), so ord F is a power of two. The same is true if exactly one of U,V is identically zero. Both cannot be zero unless F is identically zero.

If both have order q=2^r, (4.1) starts in order 2q. If its leading coefficient does not cancel, ord F=2q. If it cancels, then



$$
\operatorname{ord}F'=\operatorname{ord}V^2=2q
$$



forces a nonzero coefficient in degree 2q+1. There is no degree between 2q and 2q+1. Therefore ord F=2q+1 exactly.

Restoring the extracted Frobenius power gives



$$
\operatorname{ord}F=2^a\quad\text{or}\quad 2^s(2^{r+1}+1),
$$



which is precisely the two-bit assertion. This proves the lemma.

## 5. Applying contact to the actual finite code

Suppose m>1. By Section 2, the two term differences of A form a basis of the Sylow plane modulo two. The corresponding monomial-coordinate matrix has odd determinant and is invertible over Z_2. Its inverse expresses every original normalized group monomial as a monomial with 2-adic exponents in the two A-terms.

This formal assertion requires no choice of an odd-root logarithm: take all 2-adic powers on units with constant term 1; keep the odd-character constants separately in K.

Rescale the two A-terms by their nonzero character values, calling them X,Y. Then A=1+X+Y. On its smooth curve, Y=1+X. The other check b is a sum of exactly three monomial units, though the sum can be identically zero. Lemma 3 therefore gives a two-bit finite contact order for b.

Every period restricts to a binomial on this curve. Its finite vanishing order is a power of two. More specifically, if the Sylow orders are M,N, the two restricted periods have orders M,N: the inverse coordinate matrix has primitive rows modulo two, so the underlying normalized coordinate binomial has a simple zero, and taking its M-th or N-th Frobenius power gives the stated order.

It follows that



$$
m=\min\{\operatorname{ord}b,M,N\}.
\tag{5.1}
$$



If b is identically zero, omit its order from the minimum. Taking the minimum of a two-bit integer and powers of two preserves the two-bit property. This proves (1.2), including m<=min(M,N). If the two check gradients were independent, m=1 already.

Thus the proof applies to arbitrary term exponents and to both one-sided and two-sided repeated-root rectangular BB codes, once the support-generated component is used. It does not assume one of the checks was initially 1+x+y in the given rectangular coordinates.

## 6. Exact projection and transfer, including torsion-changing quotients

An order-two quotient of a finite abelian group with at most two cyclic Sylow factors can be put in diagonal form by a group automorphism. To see this, for C_{2^a} x C_{2^b}, a>=b, a nonzero involution is either on the first factor, on the second factor, or their sum. If a=b the usual invertible binary change of generators suffices. If a>b, adding the appropriate multiple of the first generator to the second sends the mixed involution to the second-factor involution. The odd-order part is unaffected by the quotient.

Hence at a shared odd-character point the local rings may be written



$$
\widetilde R=P/(F^2,G),\qquad R=P/(F,G).
\tag{6.1}
$$



The same ambient checks A,B are used, with their base reductions giving the actual base checks. This includes changed-polynomial, term-matched lifts. Choose A smooth in the cover ambient ring, as in Section 2, and set D=P/(A), b=B mod A, f=F mod A, g=G mod A.

The two finite homologies are represented by



$$
K(b,f^2,g;D)\longrightarrow K(b,f,g;D).
$$



The degree-one comparison map is diag(1,f,1), because the relation F^2 maps to F times the relation F. This is a chain-level comparison of free resolutions; it is not tensoring universal H_1 across a zerodivisor.

Reduce the pair (b,g), by a change of generators common to base and cover, to (c,0). We obtain



$$
K(c,f^2;D)\otimes K(0;D)
\longrightarrow K(c,f;D)\otimes K(0;D).
\tag{6.2}
$$



Let



$$
h=\operatorname{ord}f,\quad j=\operatorname{ord}c,
\quad m=\min(j,h),\quad m_+=\min(j,2h),
\tag{6.3}
$$



with the evident interpretation if f or c is zero. Write d_1=gcd(c,f) and d_2=gcd(c,f^2).

For K(c,f), the degree-one generator is (f/d_1,c/d_1), modulo its multiples by d_1. The corresponding cover generator is (f^2/d_2,c/d_2). Under diag(1,f), it maps to



$$
\frac{f d_1}{d_2}\,(f/d_1,c/d_1).
\tag{6.4}
$$



The other H_1 summand comes from H_0(K(c,f)) times the zero Koszul generator, and its map is ordinary reduction. Thus, up to units, the two coefficients are 1 and t^{h+m-m_+}.

If m_+=m, the latter coefficient vanishes modulo t^m, giving (u,v)->(u,0). If m_+>m, then m=h and m_+<=2m. The cover length is now greater than one, so a basis-parity check can be chosen and h is a power of two by Section 5. The two-bit property of m_+ forces



$$
m_+=m+\rho,\qquad \rho\text{ a power of two},\quad 0<\rho\le m.
$$



Take the normalized coordinate F's root as the DVR parameter, so f=t^m in the growing case. Equation (6.4) gives the second coefficient t^{m-rho}. This proves (1.3).

For completeness, the transfer comparison on the two period resolutions has degree-zero coefficient F and degree-one coefficients 1 on the F generator and F on the G generator. After eliminating A, its coefficients on the triple are (f,1,f). On the two H_1 summands of (6.2) these are f and d_2/d_1. This gives (1.4), including the stable case.

The formulas explicitly verify p_* tau_*=0, im tau_*=ker p_*, and tau_* p_* equals multiplication by the deck norm. In the growing case that deck norm acts as t^m; in the stable case it acts as zero.

No compact/noncompact identification was inserted in this calculation. A compact class from a universal incidence complex can land in any subspace permitted by its actual specialization map. Determining that embedding and its Hamming geometry is still necessary for distance descent.

## 7. Sharp high-contact families and an adversarial distance check

The two-bit contact theorem cannot be strengthened to “only powers of two.” Let Q=2^r, r>=1, and define



$$
P_Q(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
1+x^{3Q+1}+y^{3Q+1},&r\text{ even},\\
1+xy^{3Q}+yx^{3Q},&r\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$



Put p(x)=x^2+x+1. Direct Frobenius identities give



$$
P_Q(x,1+x)=p(x)^Q\,[x^Q+x+(r\bmod2)].
\tag{7.1}
$$



At either root of p, the last bracket has a simple zero: its derivative is 1, and the parity of r makes its value zero. Hence the exact contact order is Q+1. Taking P_Q^{2^s} realizes contact length 2^s(Q+1), and thus every possible two-bit length, in strict trinomials. Suitable powers-of-two periods truncate no earlier. These examples can be realized over binary rectangular groups C_{3L} x C_{3L}; they are not merely extension-field coefficient examples.

An especially transparent actual double-cover family takes Q=4^j, j>=1, and



$$
(6Q,3Q)\longrightarrow(6Q,6Q),\qquad
\widetilde A=1+x+y,\qquad
\widetilde B=1+x^{3Q+1}+y^{3Q+1}.
\tag{7.2}
$$



The base checks, after reducing actual supports, are



$$
A=1+x+y,\qquad B=1+x^{3Q+1}+y.
$$



There are exactly three distinct terms in each check, and reduction matches them individually. The A-support generates both actual groups, so the Tanner components are connected.

The only active odd-character pair is x=omega,y=omega^2 and its conjugate, with omega^2+omega+1=0. The x-period gives local length bound 2Q. By (7.1), the check contact is Q+1. The base y-period restricts to



$$
(1+x)^{3Q}+1=(x p(x))^Q,
$$



of local order Q, and the cover period squares it. Thus



$$
m=Q,\qquad m_+=Q+1,
$$



and the exact dimensions and lengths are



$$
n=36Q^2,\ k=4Q;\qquad n_2=72Q^2,\ k_2=4(Q+1).
\tag{7.3}
$$



The partial projection is



$$
N_{Q+1}^2\longrightarrow N_Q^2,
\qquad (u,v)\longmapsto(u\bmod t^Q,t^{Q-1}v\bmod t^Q).
\tag{7.4}
$$



This is NOT a degradation family. In fact the entire base distance is exactly four for every Q.

Let s=x^{3Q}, e=1+s. Then s has order two,



$$
B=A+xe,\qquad e^2=0,
$$



and W=(e,e) is a weight-four cycle. If it were a boundary, (Br,Ar)=(e,e) would give er=0. The group algebra is free over the involution subgroup algebra, so Ann(e)=eR. Write r=ev. Then Ar=e implies Av+1 belongs to eR, impossible at an active common maximal ideal. Hence W is a nonboundary.

There are no weight-two cycles. Same-block weight-two cycles would make a check's three-point support invariant under a nonidentity translation; for these supports this is impossible, as their projections onto the y-coordinate have the multiplicity pattern two at 0 and one at 1, with period greater than two, forcing the translation to vanish. A cross-block weight-two cycle would require the two check supports to be translates. The same y-count forces zero y-shift and the unique y-term then forces zero x-shift; the two distinct x-exponents show the supports are not equal. Odd-weight cycles are excluded by augmentation. Therefore d=4 exactly.

Upstairs A^2=B^2, and the weight-six cycle (A,B) is nontrivial uniformly. Here is a separate proof. Put e=1+x^{3Q}, f=1+y^{3Q}, and C=A+B=ex+fy. The two involutions are independent, and the group algebra is free over their subgroup algebra. Its (e,f)-associated graded ring is (R/(e,f))[e,f]/(e^2,f^2). Multiplication by the linear initial form of C is exact (kernel equals image): change e to ex+fy, whose coefficient x is a unit. The finite filtration lifts this identity to Ann(C)=CR. If (A,B)=(Br,Ar), then C(r+1)=0, so r+1=Cs. The equation Ar=B then gives C(As+1)=0, hence As+1 belongs to CR, impossible at a common maximal ideal of A,C. This proves d_2<=6 without a distance search. For Q=4 the same cycle is also checked directly by binary boundary-span membership. No exact cover distance is claimed.

Thus unbounded local nilpotent depth and arbitrarily high contact Q+1 can coexist with an independent constant-weight base logical. This is a uniform rejection of this candidate mechanism, not extrapolation from small instances.

## 8. Why the new classification is not yet a metric theorem

The basis changes in Sections 3 and 6 involve Fourier decomposition, formal monomial inverses, and Bezout/Smith operations in a DVR. None has a proven uniform Hamming operator norm. They cannot be used to claim d<=C w^2 just because a kernel vector has a simple coordinate description.

There is a second exact guard. At the active x=1 point of the inherited four-term vanishing-ratio family, the local base has H_0=F_2 and the cover has H_0=F_2[t]/t^2. Their H_1 modules and projection have exactly the full-doubling form m=1,rho=1 of Theorem 2. In local Sylow coordinates one can use



$$
\widetilde A=(1+x)(1+y),\qquad
\widetilde B=y(1+x),
$$



over F_2[C_2 x C_2], projected by y->1. Direct matrices give base m=1, cover m_+=2, and surjective rank-two projection on H_1.

The physical four-term family has growing base distance and cover distance two, as given in the starting state. Consequently, **the abstract modules and the three projection forms, without their strict-trinomial physical realization, cannot imply any universal metric bound**. This is not re-solving the four-term problem; it is a guard against an invalid use of the new normal form.

The new reduction is therefore specific: in actual BB codes, arbitrary multivariable local nilpotence is no longer an unclassified possibility. The remaining issue is the cost of realizing, in the original monomial basis, a nonzero base class when a short compact cover class occupies the kernel in (1.5). That cost may use any base sector and boundary adjustments; it must not be tied to one prescribed transferred class.

## 9. Exact verification

Run `./run_all.sh` with Python 3. Only the standard library is used. All ranks, nullspaces, field operations, polynomial factorizations, and boundary tests are exact.

* `verify_contact.py` performs square-free decomposition by binary polynomial gcd and division, independently of the local chain-matrix code. It checks every 0..8 exponent quadruple and 4,096 signed random quadruples in -96..96. There are 10,649 nonzero rational-function cases and 8 identically zero cases, which are correctly excluded. It reconstructs each polynomial from its square-free factors and verifies that every multiplicity has binary weight at most two. It also checks (7.1) and exact multiplicity Q+1 for Q=2,4,...,256.
* `verify_local.py` checks 384 support-generated local double covers over F_4 and F_8, with Sylow sizes through 16 x 8. The direct group-algebra matrices compute H_0 and H_1; radical filtrations verify their asserted module types. The projection's rank and the full radical profiles of its kernel and image agree with Theorem 2. Contact formulas are not used to construct these matrices.
* `verify_structured.py` checks six partial-growth cases 2->3, 4->6, 8->12, 4->5, 8->10, 8->9, with the predicted kernel profiles. It separately checks 72 cyclic-Sylow cases through C_64, all with m=m_+=1.
* `verify_family.py` checks the actual rectangular instance Q=4: the base has n=576,k=16,d=4, and the cover has n=1152,k=20 with a checked weight-six nonboundary. The binary rank of projection on homology is 10. Base distance four is certified by the displayed logical and the absence of every weight-two cycle, together with evenness. It also checks the four-term local-normal-form guard.

Thus there are 462 local matrix cases in addition to the actual rectangular-code check. The computations are consistency checks of the uniform arguments, not a fixed-distance presentation enumeration. They neither certify a new degradation ratio nor exhaust the metric obstruction. The new argument has not been externally refereed or proof-assistant formalized.

## 10. Retained bounds and the next precise lemma

The universal quadratic distance inequality is neither established nor refuted. No strict-trinomial family with d->infinity and d_2/d->0 is constructed here.

The inherited bounds remain



$$
d\le4d_2^3,
$$





$$
\gamma_-^{(3)}(N)\ge
\max\left\{\min(1/12,20/N),N^{-2/3},
\frac{\pi3^{1/6}}{2^{13/3}\sqrt N}\right\}.
$$



The retained exact upper construction has ratio 6/7 for N>=636, at its supplied certification status; its minimum-distance certificates were not rerun. The inherited cubic theorem excludes every bounded d_2 when d grows. The sharper d_2=6 => d<=56 and d_2=8 => d<=96 statements, and the failure of the two-check weight-six filling lemma, are unchanged.

**Next lemma: quadratic physical replacement for the local kernel.** Let Y be a minimum nonboundary of weight w in a valid strict-trinomial BB double cover. Suppose its incidence presentation is mixed finite/strip with visible 2-primary torsion and pY is a base boundary. In the actual finite local normal forms, every component of [Y] then lies in



$$
(t^mN_{m+\rho})\oplus(t^\rho N_{m+\rho}),
$$



where rho=0, or m and rho are powers of two with 0<rho<=m. Prove or refute that there is an absolute C and a **physical binary base cycle**, in any nonzero base homology class, of weight at most C w^2.

The new theorem supplies the complete finite-local algebra entering this statement. A proof still needs a sparse realization argument for the original monomial chains, not a bound on the sizes or lengths of the abstract modules. Together with the inherited cases, this replacement lemma is sufficient for universal quadratic descent.
