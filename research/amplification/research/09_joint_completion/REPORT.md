# Joint completion at primitive slope (2,1)

## STATUS: CONSTANT_BOUND_STILL_OPEN

Neither the half-weight completion lemma nor the full compact factor-three inequality is proved or refuted in this round. No larger amplification ratio is claimed. The inherited slope-(2,1) factor-four bound remains the available uniform bound from this route.

The new structural results concern the **full** compact optimization, not one chosen lift:

1. Every homology message and every boundary adjustment are retained in an exact signed-cut formulation. The graph has two odd step lengths and is bipartite. Half of the entire boundary field can be minimized pointwise by a six-vote rule, leaving an explicit unrestricted optimization over the message and the other half-field.
2. In a minimum-support optimal boundary field, each connected component has at most as many independent graph cycles as source vertices. A source-free component must be a tree with prescribed edge signs. For each fixed message, an explicit finite neighborhood is guaranteed to contain a global optimum; this is not a heuristic support cutoff.
3. The supplied record example has the complete projection/completion Pareto frontier {(12,1),(14,0)}. The second point is supplied here by a new attaining word. Both points have physical weight 14; the lower bounds are inherited exact certificates, re-audited this round.
4. A tempting restricted divided-Euler/majority bound is false even on a circuit that is a strict local minimum under every single stabilizer flip. The explicit example is a **base boundary**, not a minimum logical or an amplification counterexample. It demonstrates that circuit structure and single-stabilizer stability are insufficient hypotheses for that route.

All weights below are universal finite Laurent-support weights unless explicitly stated otherwise. The supplied finite-specialization and large-period-realization theorems remain inputs. Their relevant nonboundary criterion is also derived directly below.

## 1. Setting and the inherited compact labels

Let P=F_2[z,z^{-1}], and put

\[
X=z^r,\quad Y=z^{2s},\quad H=z^{2g},\quad r\text{ odd},\ g>0,
\]
\[
A=1+X+Y,\qquad B=1+XH^2+YH.
\]

Assume strict trinomials and primitive check differences. The universal cover is over P[epsilon]/(epsilon^2), with t=1+epsilon and

\[
\widehat A=A+\epsilon=t+X+Y,\qquad
\widehat B=B+\epsilon=t+XH^2+YH.
\]

Use the factorization from the supplied report:

\[
e=\gcd(A,1+z^g),\qquad j=\gcd(A,1+XH),\qquad c=\gcd(A,B)=ej,
\]
\[
\gcd(e,j)=1,\quad a_0=A/c,\quad b_0=B/c,\quad G=e^2j=\gcd(A^2,A+B).
\]

Here A is square-free, since its derivative is a Laurent monomial. Let E_0 be a nonconstant divisor of e. In the reduced hard-core finite specialization, the base compact image and the cover compact image are both P/(E_0). The base minimum is

\[
\Delta(E_0)=\min_{E_0\nmid f}(|b_0f|+|a_0f|).
\]

The desired full inequality is \(\widehat\Delta(E_0)\le3\Delta(E_0)\).

The exact specialization condition is important. A compatible finite quotient imposes
\(\rho=z^L+1+\kappa\epsilon\), with \(\kappa\in\{0,1\}\). Multiplication by rho is injective on the free chain modules: its P-linear determinant is \((z^L+1)^2\). If a universal cycle becomes a boundary, write it as \(\partial y+\rho x\). Applying the differential and cancelling rho shows x is a cycle. Thus the image of universal H_1 is exactly its quotient by rho, not an assumed identification with all finite homology.

On universal H_1, epsilon acts as A. Therefore the compact image is

\[
P/\gcd(G,z^L+1+\kappa A).
\]

In the hard core this divisor is E_0. Finite homology can have an additional specialization-created sector; none of the compact formulas below identifies or discards that sector by an unjustified base-change argument.

## 2. A basis for all universal cover cycles

Define

\[
P_0=\frac{AB+A+B}{G},\qquad
S_0=\frac{A+B}{G},\qquad
Q_0=\frac{A^2}{G}.
\]

Every universal cover cycle has, uniquely, the following form in physical sheet coordinates:

\[
\boxed{
(U_0,U_1,V_0,V_1)=
(P_0\phi+(B+1)h,\ S_0\phi+h,\ Q_0\phi+(A+1)h,\ h),
\quad \phi,h\in P.
}
\]

This includes all messages and all boundary adjustments. For a direct proof, write the cycle in epsilon coordinates as
\((u_0+\epsilon u_1,v_0+\epsilon v_1)\). Its equations are

\[
Au_0+Bv_0=0,\qquad u_0+v_0+Au_1+Bv_1=0.
\]

Set \(r_1=u_1+v_1\), \(s_1=v_0+Av_1\). Then
\(A^2r_1+(A+B)s_1=0\). The two coefficients divided by G are coprime in P, so

\[
r_1=S_0\phi,\qquad s_1=Q_0\phi.
\]

Putting h=v_1 and recovering the other variables gives the boxed formula, after converting from epsilon to physical t coordinates.

A boundary generated by \(l+\epsilon k\) corresponds to

\[
\phi=Gk,\qquad h=l+Ak.
\]

Thus universal H_1 is P/(G), with coordinate phi. In the selected finite compact image, a displayed cycle is nontrivial **if and only if** E_0 does not divide phi. In particular, the complete minimum is

\[
\widehat\Delta(E_0)=\min_{E_0\nmid\phi}\min_{h\in P}
\left(|P_0\phi+(B+1)h|+|S_0\phi+h|
+|Q_0\phi+(A+1)h|+|h|\right).
\]

There is no restriction on the universal j-component, the extra e-primary layer, or either boundary coefficient.

## 3. Exact signed-cut normal form

Set

\[
\ell=r-2s,\qquad m=\ell+2g.
\]

Both are odd and nonzero. Since

\[
A+1=Y(1+z^\ell),\qquad B+1=YH(1+z^m),
\]

define, for each message phi,

\[
\alpha=(YH)^{-1}P_0\phi,\quad
\beta=Y^{-1}Q_0\phi,\quad
\sigma=S_0\phi.
\]

Monomial shifts preserve weight. Therefore the exact physical energy is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_\phi(h)=
|\alpha+(1+z^m)h|+|\beta+(1+z^\ell)h|
+|\sigma+h|+|h|.
}
\]

The full compact minimum is \(\min_{E_0\nmid\phi,h}\mathcal E_\phi(h)\).

Interpret the coefficient h_i as a binary spin at integer vertex i. A type-m edge indexed by i joins i to i-m and has prescribed parity alpha_i. A type-l edge indexed by i joins i to i-l and has prescribed parity beta_i. An edge costs one when the endpoint xor differs from its prescribed parity. At each vertex there are two unary costs, against sigma_i and against zero.

The graph is a degree-four multigraph, counting the two edge types separately. It is bipartite because both steps are odd. Negative steps, parallel geometric edges, and disconnected congruence classes are permitted. This is a graph on the **universal integer coordinate**. No assertion is made that a small finite-period quotient preserves this bipartition.

Writing F for the prescribed-parity-one edges, the energy also reads

\[
\mathcal E_\phi(h)=|F\mathbin\triangle\delta(\operatorname{supp}h)|
+|\sigma|+2|\operatorname{supp}h\setminus\operatorname{supp}\sigma|.
\]

Here delta is the edge cut. Source vertices are precisely the support of sigma: their unary contribution is one regardless of h_i; all other vertices cost two when h_i=1.

### The edge signs and sources are not independent

The BB data obey the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
(1+z^\ell)\alpha+(1+z^m)\beta
=Y^{-2}H^{-1}\sigma.
}
\]

Indeed,

\[
(A+1)(AB+A+B)+(B+1)A^2=A+B.
\]

The left side of the boxed identity is the parity around an elementary square of the two-step graph. Moreover, alpha, beta, and sigma are all fixed polynomial multiples of the **same** message phi. Even the displayed square-parity identity is weaker than this full common-message requirement. A counterexample built from arbitrary graph signs is not automatically a BB counterexample.

## 4. Exact elimination of half the boundary variables

Fix a finitely supported even field \(h_{\rm ev}\). At an odd vertex i, all four neighbors are even. Its four edge votes and two unary votes are

\[
\alpha_i+h_{i-m},\quad
\alpha_{i+m}+h_{i+m},\quad
\beta_i+h_{i-\ell},\quad
\beta_{i+\ell}+h_{i+\ell},\quad
\sigma_i,\quad0.
\]

Let k_i be the number of ones in these six votes. Assigning h_i=0 costs k_i; assigning h_i=1 costs 6-k_i. Different odd vertices have disjoint sets of edges, so their decisions are independent. Choosing h_i=1 exactly when k_i>3, with either choice at ties, gives the optimum.

Consequently the entire unrestricted compact optimization is

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Delta(E_0)=
\min_{\substack{E_0\nmid\phi\\h_{\rm ev}\in P_{\rm ev}}}
\left[
\sum_{i\ {m even}}\bigl((\sigma_i\oplus h_i)+h_i\bigr)
+\sum_{i\ {m odd}}\min(k_i,6-k_i)
\right].
}
\]

All sums are finite. Outside a finite neighborhood of the sources, prescribed-parity-one edges, and retained even support, all votes are zero.

This eliminates half of the **entire** boundary field, for every message. It is distinct from optimizing a chosen divided-Euler representative inside a restricted boundary family. The remaining message and even field are still unrestricted. No claim is made that their joint minimization is solved, or that repeated greedy majority updates find its global optimum.

The elimination minimizes physical weight. It need not preserve separate caps on projected weight and completion cost. The half-weight lemma remains a stronger constrained problem; Section 7 records those constraints exactly.

## 5. Global structure of an optimal boundary field

For fixed phi, an optimum exists because the nonnegative integer energy has a minimum among finitely supported fields. Choose an optimum with the fewest nonzero h_i.

Let R be a connected component of its support. Write

\[
n_R=|R|,\quad E(R)=\text{number of internal edges, with multiplicity},
\quad b_1(R)=E(R)-n_R+1,
\]

and let \(q_R=|R\cap\operatorname{supp}\sigma|\). Removing the component changes the energy by

\[
I(R)=2|F\cap\delta R|-|\delta R|-2|R\setminus\operatorname{supp}\sigma|.
\]

Optimality and minimum support imply I(R)>0. Its parity is even, since the graph has degree four; hence I(R)>=2. Degree counting gives

\[
|\delta R|=4n_R-2E(R)=2n_R+2-2b_1(R).
\]

Using \(|F\cap\delta R|\le|\delta R|\) yields

\[
2\le I(R)\le2(q_R+1-b_1(R)),
\]

so

\[
\boxed{b_1(R)\le q_R.}
\]

In particular, a source-free component must be a tree and all its cut edges must have prescribed parity one. Its internal edges must all have prescribed parity zero. To prove the latter assertion, suppose an internal tree edge had parity one and remove one of the two pieces separated by that edge. The removed vertices have no source. Every other edge leaving the piece was a parity-one cut edge. The energy change is exactly zero, contradicting minimum support among optima.

This is a global removal argument, not merely a single-spin necessary condition. It does **not** bound the number of vertices in a tree component, or relate the source size to the minimum base distance. Those are genuine remaining gaps.

### A rigorous finite domain for each fixed message

Let

\[
Q=|\alpha|+|\beta|,\qquad S=|\sigma|,\qquad K=S+\lfloor Q/2\rfloor.
\]

At h=0 the energy is Q+S. At any field it is at least
\(S+2|\operatorname{supp}h\setminus\operatorname{supp}\sigma|\). Thus an optimum has support size at most K.

Every component of a minimum-support optimum intersects the finite anchor set consisting of the source vertices and the endpoints of all prescribed-parity-one edges. Otherwise removing it strictly lowers the energy. Since the component has at most K vertices, it lies within K-1 graph steps of that anchor set.

Therefore, for each fixed message, a global boundary optimum exists in this explicitly finite neighborhood. A conservative ordinary-coordinate radius is

\[
(K-1)\max(|\ell|,|m|).
\]

When K=0 the optimum is h=0. This gives a rigorous way to certify an all-boundary fixed-message minimum without an arbitrary support cutoff. It does not bound the message phi and therefore is not, by itself, a full compact-distance algorithm with a finite preset message range.

## 6. A local-majority shortcut is false, even on a strict local circuit minimum

A separate constructive route began with the divided-Euler lift and optimized one parity-restricted boundary family. This is **not** the full optimization of Section 4.

Write \(Y=y^2,H=Z^2\). For a base cycle (u,v), split parity and take square roots:

\[
a=\sqrt{u_{\rm even}},\quad b=\sqrt{X u_{\rm odd}},\quad
c_1=\sqrt{v_{\rm even}},\quad d_1=\sqrt{X v_{\rm odd}}.
\]

The two parity equations are

\[
(1+y)a+b+(1+yZ)c_1+Z^2d_1=0,
\]
\[
Xa+(1+y)b+XZ^2c_1+(1+yZ)d_1=0.
\]

Put J=1+Z+yZ. After monomial shifts of the physical channels, the divided-Euler lift with arbitrary even boundary addition eta^2 has six outputs

\[
Z^{-2}Ja+\eta,\quad Z^{-1}a+b+\eta,\quad
ya+(1+Z)b+\eta,
\]
\[
Jc_1+\eta,\quad c_1+Zd_1+\eta,\quad
yc_1+(1+Z)d_1+\eta.
\]

Thus this subfamily also has a pointwise six-vote minimum, denoted M(a,b,c_1,d_1). Translating the input by an odd monomial replaces the four arguments, up to a common monomial, by

\[
(b,Xa,d_1,Xc_1).
\]

It was tempting to assert that the smaller of these two costs is always at most three times the input cycle weight. This is false, even with circuit support and strict single-stabilizer local minimality.

### An exact counterexample to that restricted assertion

In the formal Laurent ring with variables X,y,Z, define

\[
\mathcal A=1+X+y^2,\qquad
\mathcal B=1+XZ^4+y^2Z^2,
\]
\[
F=\mathcal A^3\mathcal B^3
\left[X^2+(1+Xy^4)\mathcal A^4\right].
\]

Take the base boundary

\[
z_{\rm bad}=(\mathcal B F,\mathcal A F).
\]

Exact sparse arithmetic gives

\[
|z_{\rm bad}|=182,\qquad
M(a,b,c_1,d_1)=M(b,Xa,d_1,Xc_1)=550>546=3|z_{\rm bad}|.
\]

This specializes without the relevant support collisions to the genuine primitive slope-(2,1) data

\[
X=z,\qquad y=z^{30001},\qquad Z=z^{303}.
\]

Here r=1, s=30001, g=303, and E_0=1+z+z^2 is a nontrivial resonance divisor. The verifier independently checks the specialized polynomial identities and all physical lift equations.

There are 182 selected columns and their exact binary rank is 181. Since their total syndrome is zero, the support is a circuit. Every translate of the six-term base boundary overlaps this circuit in at most two coordinates. Thus every single monomial stabilizer flip increases its weight by at least two.

Nevertheless this is a **boundary**, explicitly witnessed by F. It is not a minimum nonboundary and is not a counterexample to the half-weight lemma or the full factor-three inequality. Its purpose is more limited and precise: the local parity equations, circuit support, and single-stabilizer stability do not suffice to justify the restricted-majority inequality. A successful proof along that route must use genuine global minimality or additional nonlocal boundary moves.

There is no inconsistency with the exact full half-field elimination. That elimination applies to every message and every even boundary field; the false assertion fixes a much narrower set derived from one input cycle.

## 7. Exact Pareto frontier for the supplied record

Use the supplied slope-(2,1) seed X=z, Y=z^{-4}, H=z^6. After gauging the first check by z^4,

\[
A_*=1+z^4+z^5,\qquad B_*=1+z^2+z^{13},
\]
\[
\widehat A_*=1+z^5+t z^4,\qquad
\widehat B_*=z^2+z^{13}+t.
\]

The supplied report proves, with exact unrestricted-length trellis certificates,

\[
\Delta=6,\qquad\min\{|w|:w\text{ is a surviving liftable base projection}\}=12,
\qquad\widehat\Delta=14.
\]

The three lower certificates used here were re-audited. They cover arbitrary finite Laurent lengths by sublevel closure under every trellis transition, not by a support truncation.

The previously supplied weight-fourteen word has supports

\[
U_0=\{3,10,11\},\quad
U_1=\{0,4,6,7,9,11,12\},\quad
V_0=\{0\},\quad V_1=\{2,3,4\}.
\]

Its projected weight is 12 and its outside-support cost is one.

A new word of the same physical weight has

\[
U_0=\{4,12,20\},\qquad U_1=\{0,5,16,19,21\},
\]
\[
V_0=\{0,4,10\},\qquad V_1=\{2,6,13\}.
\]

The two sheets in each block are disjoint. Its projected weight is 14 and its completion cost is zero. Both words satisfy both universal cover equations exactly. With e=1+z+z^2 and j=1+z+z^3, their projected right component is divisible by j and nonzero modulo e. The intrinsic cover coordinate can also be checked through

\[
W=z^8(V_0+V_1)+z^4A_*V_1,\qquad
\phi=W/j\pmod{e^2j}.
\]

In both cases j divides W and phi is nonzero modulo e. A separate finite-matrix/dual-functional audit at L=201 verifies the new word is a nonboundary in the actual cover, not just in universal homology.

The **complete nondominated frontier** of projected weight and outside-support cost is

\[
\boxed{\{(12,1),(14,0)\}.}
\]

Proof of completeness: every surviving compact projection has weight at least 12, all projected cycles have even weight, and every full surviving compact cover word has physical weight at least 14. A pair with positive completion cost is dominated by (12,1); a pair with zero completion cost must have projected weight at least 14 and is dominated by (14,0). Both points are attained.

Thus minimizing completion cost first selects a projection too large for the half-weight lemma's projected-weight cap 2 Delta=12. The correct witness for that lemma in this example uses one outside-support coordinate. Conversely, fixing projected weight at its minimum is not the only way to attain the physical minimum. This is an exact all-message Pareto statement, not an inference from one chosen generator.

The minimum fourteen, the ratio 7/3, and the corresponding large-period actual distances are inherited results, not new records. The new data are the zero-extra attaining word and the resulting complete Pareto-frontier deduction.

## 8. Exact form of the still-unproved half-weight lemma

For the full parameterization, the projection is

\[
p_{\phi,h}=\frac{AB}{G}\phi+Bh,
\qquad q_{\phi,h}=\frac{A^2}{G}\phi+Ah.
\]

Its base message is

\[
f_{\rm proj}=(A/e)\phi+ch=ja_0\phi+ch.
\]

Let f_0 be a message of a minimum surviving base cycle. Since P_e is one modulo e and zero modulo j, the condition that this projection equal P_e(b_0 f_0,a_0 f_0) plus a **universal base boundary** is exactly

\[
\boxed{ja_0\phi\equiv f_0\pmod e.}
\]

It is not enough to impose only a congruence modulo E_0 when E_0 is a proper divisor of e. This is one reason the sufficient lemma is stronger than the full compact factor-three assertion.

Writing \(\mathcal P(\phi,h)=|p_{\phi,h}|+|q_{\phi,h}|\), the half-weight lemma is precisely the existence, for some minimum f_0, of phi,h satisfying

\[
ja_0\phi\equiv f_0\pmod e,\qquad
\mathcal P(\phi,h)\le2\Delta,\qquad
\mathcal E_\phi(h)-\mathcal P(\phi,h)\le\Delta.
\]

The difference in the last condition is twice the outside-support cost of that filling. The full factor-three problem drops the class-pinning congruence, keeps only E_0 not dividing phi, and minimizes physical energy.

The six-vote rule exactly minimizes physical energy over the odd field for fixed phi and even field. It does not necessarily minimize the two costs separately or preserve their two caps. No such claim is used.

### A useful but still insufficient toggle criterion

For a fixed projection w and filling r, suppose all filling support outside w is contained in the support of a base cycle z' of weight at most Delta. Both r and r+z' are valid fillings. Outside w, the two fillings are complementary on the relevant part of z' and vanish elsewhere. Their average cost is at most Delta/2. Therefore one meets the half-weight completion budget.

This criterion is exact. A universal construction of the required support containment, jointly with the projected-weight cap, was not obtained. Neither an isolated filling nor a chosen sparse projector suffices to assume it.

## 10. Remaining obstruction and the next target

The full problem is now the explicit half-field inequality

\[
\boxed{
\min_{\substack{E_0\nmid\phi\\h_{\rm ev}\in P_{\rm ev}}}
\left[
\sum_{i\ {m even}}((\sigma_i\oplus h_i)+h_i)
+\sum_{i\ {m odd}}\min(k_i,6-k_i)
\right]\le3\Delta(E_0).
}
\]

The edge data in this formula must be the prescribed polynomial multiples of phi. The source/edge identity, the compact residue, and genuine global minimality of the base class are all relevant constraints.

What is controlled is the optimal choice of half the boundary variables, the cycle rank of optimal support components, and a finite domain for each fixed message. What is not controlled is the size of the tree parts or their joint behavior as the message changes. The explicit circuit-boundary control shows why circuit support and single-stabilizer local minimality alone cannot fill this gap.

**The next lemma to attack is a global boundary-exchange inequality for the common-message half-field model, using the minimum base-class hypothesis to control the tree components.** The precise numerical target is the boxed inequality above, not a majority claim for arbitrary cycles and not a bound on a preselected message. A class-pinned proof satisfying the two caps in Section 8 would prove the stronger half-weight lemma; failure of that stronger statement would still require analyzing the unrestricted boxed minimum.

A genuine full compact-minimum ratio above three would, by the supplied large-period realization theorem, give actual finite-distance amplification above three. This round has no such compact counterexample.

The signed-cut conversion and odd-field elimination use only that the two nonconstant terms of each check have opposite parity. Algebraically the same steps apply to general primitive resonance slopes, replacing m by \(r-2s+2g(p-q)\) and YH by \(YH^q\). This observation transfers the reduction, not the unproved factor-three bound. No additional slope is solved here.
