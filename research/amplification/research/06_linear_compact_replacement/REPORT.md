# Exact compact distance and linear replacement in the torsion–Frobenius normal form

## STATUS: COMPACT_FACTOR_TWO_PROVED

Research round: 6 September 2026.

The proposed factor-three compact inequality holds, and can be sharpened to the optimal factor two:

\[
\boxed{\widetilde\delta_{\rm comp}\le2\delta_{\rm comp}.}
\]

More precisely, the ratio is exactly one of

\[
\boxed{1,\quad\tfrac32,\quad2,}
\]

determined by the universal torsion extension, whenever the compact base image in the selected finite quotient is nonzero. The weights in this statement are the weights of universal finite-support representatives, as in the supplied definition of compact replacement. They are not necessarily the minimum weights after coordinates are identified in the finite quotient.

The main new theorem computes that compact minimum exactly. Write

\[
U=\mathbb Z^2\oplus T,\qquad
A=1+X+Y,\qquad B=1+hX^q+kY^q,
\]

where \(q=2^s\), \(T=\langle h,k\rangle\) is finite, and

\[
T_2\cong C_{2^a}\times C_{2^b},\qquad a\ge b\ge0,
\qquad o=|T_{\rm odd}|.
\]

Trivial cyclic factors are allowed. Then

\[
\boxed{\delta_\infty
=o\,2^{a-b}3^b(3^s+1).}
\]

Here \(\delta_\infty\) is the minimum weight of a universal nonboundary. A representative attaining this minimum generates the entire universal first homology module. Consequently, in every finite quotient with nonzero compact image,

\[
\boxed{\delta_{\rm comp}=\delta_\infty.}
\]

The new argument gives a lower bound for every representative and every nonzero universal class, not just for the canonical power generator. The upper bound uses an explicitly optimized generator and includes an explicit boundary adjustment from the previous generator.

The all-code constant question remains open. This result settles the genuinely higher-rank, non-finite-check regime identified in the supplied normal-form theorem; it does not assert \(d_2\le2d\) for arbitrary BB double covers. The established \(6\to14\) example is outside this regime.

## 1. Scope, input, and definitions

The supplied `BB_compact_survival_report.md` establishes the following input: a connected incidence presentation generated by the four check differences, of free rank at least two, with neither check supported in a finite subgroup, has nonzero compact first homology only in the torsion–Frobenius normal form displayed above. It also establishes compact survival for compatible double covers in that form. The proof below does not repeat the incidence classification. It gives a new metric analysis of that normal form.

Let

\[
S=\mathbb F_2[T][X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}],
\]

with the physical monomial basis. The complex has cycle equation \(Au+Bv=0\) and boundaries \((Bf,Af)\). All universal chains have finite support.

For a fixed quotient \(U\twoheadrightarrow G\), define

\[
\delta_{\rm comp}(G)
=\min\{|u|+|v|:\ (u,v)\text{ is a universal cycle and its finite image is nontrivial}\}.
\]

The number is defined only when the compact image is nonzero. Define the cover quantity in the same way for a fixed compatible universal double cover. Additional finite quotient relations, torsion collapse, and finite even-period or nonsplit covers are allowed.

There are three distinct quantities:

* the unrestricted universal minimum \(\delta_\infty\);
* the universal representative weight \(\delta_{\rm comp}(G)\), with the extra survival condition;
* the actual finite quantum distance \(d(G)\).

We prove equality of the first two whenever the latter is defined. The finite distance can be smaller, because specialization can merge coordinates or create additional homology.

Both \(A\) and \(B\) are non-zero-divisors in \(S\): their leading coefficients as polynomials in \(Y\) are units. For a universal cycle,

\[
(u,v)\text{ is a boundary}\iff v\in(A)
\iff u\in(B).
\]

For example, if \(v=Af\), then \(A(u+Bf)=0\); regularity of \(A\) gives \(u=Bf\). This fact is crucial for the two lower bounds below.

## 2. A generic finite-group kernel has an exact Hamming distance

Let \(T=\langle h,k\rangle\) be any finite abelian group generated by two elements. Let \(\alpha\) be transcendental over \(\mathbb F_2\), and work over a field containing \(\mathbb F_2(\alpha)\). Put

\[
D=1+\alpha h+(1+\alpha)k.
\]

Assume any constant-field extension still treats \(\alpha\) as transcendental. Let Hamming weight count nonzero coefficients in the group basis of the group algebra.

**Kernel-distance lemma.** If

\[
T_2\cong C_{2^a}\times C_{2^b},\quad a\ge b,
\quad o=|T_{\rm odd}|,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{d\bigl(\operatorname{ann}(D)\bigr)=o\,2^{a-b}3^b.}
\]

### 2.1 Lower bound by squaring and cosets

Let \(T^{(2)}=\{t^2:t\in T\}\). Since

\[
D^2=1+\alpha^2h^2+(1+\alpha)^2k^2
\]

lies in the group algebra of \(T^{(2)}\), every coset component of a vector annihilated by \(D\) is annihilated by \(D^2\). Thus every nonzero component has weight at least the corresponding kernel distance on \(T^{(2)}\).

When \([T:T^{(2)}]=4\), the images of \(h,k\) form a basis of \(C_2^2\). A nonzero \(D\)-annihilated vector must occupy at least three cosets. Indeed, each occupied coset contributes, under multiplication by \(D\), to three distinct quotient positions. For one or two occupied cosets there is a quotient position reached from exactly one of them, with a monomial-unit coefficient. That contribution cannot vanish. Therefore one squaring step contributes a factor of at least three.

When \([T:T^{(2)}]=2\), a nonzero vector must occupy both cosets. If precisely one of \(h,k\) is odd modulo squares, its term has a monomial-unit coefficient in the opposite coset. If both are odd, the identity term is the unique contribution to the original coset. Either case rules out a vector supported in just one coset. This step contributes a factor of at least two.

There are \(b\) steps of index four and \(a-b\) steps of index two.

At the end only the odd-order subgroup remains. Its group algebra can be split into characters after extending the constant field. The eigenvalue in a character \(\chi\) is

\[
(1+\chi(k))+(\chi(h)+\chi(k))\alpha.
\]

Transcendence of \(\alpha\) implies that this vanishes only when \(\chi(h)=\chi(k)=1\). Since \(h,k\) generate the group, only the trivial character contributes. The kernel is the span of the odd norm and has distance \(o\).

Multiplying the factors gives the lower bound \(o2^{a-b}3^b\). Only the odd subgroup was diagonalized; no semisimplicity is assumed for the two-primary subgroup.

### 2.2 Attaining the bound

Set \(M=2^a\), \(m=2^b\). After exchanging \(h,k\) if needed, the two-primary component \(h_2\) has order \(M\). There is a decomposition

\[
T_2=\langle h_2\rangle\times\langle w\rangle,
\qquad |w|=m,\qquad k_2=h_2^j w.
\]

Put

\[
e=\sum_{t\in T_{\rm odd}}t,
\qquad
N=\sum_{i=0}^{M/m-1}h_2^{mi}.
\]

Then

\[
eN D^{m-1}\in\operatorname{ann}(D),
\]

because \(eNh^m=eNk^m=eN\) and \(1+\alpha^m+(1+\alpha)^m=0\).

The Frobenius product \(D^{m-1}\) has \(3^b\) terms. They are distinct after quotienting the two-primary group by \(\langle h_2^m\rangle\): that quotient is \(C_m^2\), and the images of \(h_2,k_2\) are a basis. Multiplication by the norm supplies \(oM/m\) distinct terms per such position. All coefficients are nonzero. Hence its weight is exactly \(o2^{a-b}3^b\), proving the lemma.

For a purely two-primary group the same proof works over any characteristic-two field with \(\alpha\ne0,1\). Transcendence is needed to exclude accidental odd-character kernels.

## 3. Two faithful generic evaluations give separate lower bounds

Write

\[
\Delta(T)=o\,2^{a-b}3^b.
\]

Let \((u,v)\) be any nonboundary universal cycle. We prove

\[
|v|\ge\Delta(T),\qquad |u|\ge3^s\Delta(T).
\]

### 3.1 The second block

Set \(K=\mathbb F_2(\xi)\), and define

\[
\Phi_A:S\longrightarrow K[T],
\qquad X\mapsto\xi,\quad Y\mapsto1+\xi,
\]

leaving torsion monomials unchanged. Its kernel is exactly \((A)\). It sends every source monomial to a scalar times one target group basis vector, so

\[
\operatorname{wt}(\Phi_A(v))\le|v|.
\]

Since \(v\notin(A)\), its image is nonzero. The cycle equation gives

\[
\bigl(1+\xi^q h+(1+\xi)^q k\bigr)\Phi_A(v)=0.
\]

Apply the kernel-distance lemma with \(\alpha=\xi^q\) to get \(|v|\ge\Delta(T)\).

### 3.2 The first block: adjoining roots of the torsion generators

Present \(T=\mathbb Z^2/L\), with the standard generators mapping to \(h,k\). Define

\[
V=\mathbb Z^2/qL,
\]

with generators \(a_0,b_0\). There is an injective homomorphism

\[
\iota:T\hookrightarrow V,
\qquad h\mapsto a_0^{-q},\quad k\mapsto b_0^{-q}.
\]

Define

\[
\Phi_B:S\longrightarrow K[V],
\qquad X\mapsto\xi a_0,
\quad Y\mapsto(1+\xi)b_0,
\quad t\mapsto\iota(t).
\]

Then \(\Phi_B(B)=0\) and

\[
\Phi_B(A)=1+\xi a_0+(1+\xi)b_0.
\]

The essential statement is

\[
\boxed{\ker\Phi_B=(B).}
\]

Here is a direct proof, avoiding any unsupported specialization-injectivity claim. The subgroup

\[
\Lambda=\langle hX^q,\ kY^q\rangle\le U
\]

is free of rank two, and \(U/\Lambda\cong V\). The source group algebra is free on its coset monomials over

\[
\mathbb F_2[\Lambda]
=\mathbb F_2[P^{\pm1},Q^{\pm1}],
\quad P=hX^q,\quad Q=kY^q.
\]

On this coefficient ring, \(\Phi_B\) evaluates

\[
P\mapsto\xi^q,\qquad Q\mapsto(1+\xi)^q=1+\xi^q.
\]

The kernel of that coefficient evaluation is precisely \((1+P+Q)=(B)\). Distinct cosets map to distinct group basis vectors of \(K[V]\), each multiplied by a nonzero rational scalar. Therefore the whole map has exactly the same kernel ideal.

It follows that \(\Phi_B(u)\ne0\), because \(u\notin(B)\). It lies in the kernel of \(1+\xi a_0+(1+\xi)b_0\), and its target Hamming weight is at most \(|u|\).

Multiplying the relation lattice by \(q\) multiplies both invariant factors of \(T\) by \(q\). Thus \(V\) has the same odd subgroup order and two-primary invariant factors

\[
2^{a+s},\qquad2^{b+s}.
\]

The kernel-distance lemma gives

\[
|u|\ge\Delta(V)=3^s\Delta(T).
\]

Combining the two blocks proves

\[
\boxed{|u|+|v|\ge(3^s+1)\Delta(T)}
\]

for every universal nonboundary, after arbitrary boundary adjustment and in every homology sector.

These generic evaluations are algebraic tools over a transcendental parameter. They are not the finite quotient maps appearing in the BB cover problem. Their exact kernels were proved explicitly.

## 4. A shortest representative generates the entire compact module

Use the notation \(M=2^a\), \(m=2^b\), \(e\), and \(N\) from Section 2. Define

\[
\boxed{Z_{\min}=\bigl(eN A^{qm-1},\ eN B^{m-1}\bigr).}
\]

It is a cycle, since

\[
eN B^m=eN A^{qm}.
\]

Its exact physical weights are

\[
|eN A^{qm-1}|=o\frac{M}{m}3^{s+b},
\qquad
|eN B^{m-1}|=o\frac{M}{m}3^b.
\]

There are no hidden cancellations in these counts: the free exponents in each Frobenius expansion are distinct, and the torsion norms occupy distinct coordinates. This matches the lower bound in Section 3.

We must additionally show that the minimum cycle, rather than merely some heavier generator, survives every relevant finite quotient.

The prior module calculation identifies compact homology with the annihilator of \(B\) modulo \(A\). All of this homology lies in the odd norm sector: in every other odd character, the residual image of \(B\) is a nonzero Laurent polynomial in \(X\), hence is a non-zero-divisor; exactness persists through the finite two-primary nilpotent filtration. We briefly give the exact identity needed here. After restricting to the odd norm sector, put

\[
\mathcal B=\mathbb F_2[X^{\pm1},(1+X)^{\pm1}],
\quad \alpha=X^q,\quad\beta=(1+X)^q,
\]

and

\[
D=1+\alpha h_2+\beta k_2,
\qquad \alpha+\beta=1.
\]

The nilpotent-pencil argument gives

\[
\operatorname{ann}(D)=D^{M-1}\mathcal B[T_2].
\]

For completeness, write \(u=1+h_2\), \(v=1+w\), so the ring is
\(\mathcal B[u,v]/(u^M,v^m)\). The coefficient of \(u\) in \(D\) is either \(\alpha\) or \(1\), a unit. The substitution \((u,v)\mapsto(D,v)\) is an automorphism by finite nilpotent inversion; this proves the annihilator identity and the cyclic generator statement.

More specifically, let

\[
c=\begin{cases}\alpha,&j\text{ even},\\1,&j\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]

Since \(k_2^m=h_2^{jm}\), expansion in \(z=1+h_2^m\) gives

\[
D^m=c^m z+\text{higher powers of }z.
\]

The order of nilpotence of \(z\) is \(M/m\), so

\[
\boxed{D^{M-m}=c^{M-m}N.}
\]

Therefore

\[
N D^{m-1}=c^{-(M-m)}D^{M-1}.
\]

The scalar \(c^{-(M-m)}\) is a monomial unit: it is either one or a power of \(X\). Thus the minimum cycle differs, in universal homology, from a monomial translate of the previous canonical generator

\[
e(A^{qM-1},B^{M-1}).
\]

It generates the entire universal compact module.

This also supplies a concrete boundary adjustment. After multiplication by the indicated power of \(X\), the difference of the second components is divisible by \(A\). Let that quotient be \(f\). Regularity of \(A\) and the cycle equations force the first-component difference to be \(Bf\). The resulting difference is exactly the universal boundary \((Bf,Af)\). The verification scripts reconstruct \(f\) rather than just compare module dimensions.

If a compact class survives a finite quotient, some multiple of \([Z_{\min}]\) survives. Hence \([Z_{\min}]\) itself cannot specialize to a boundary. This proves

\[
\boxed{\delta_{\rm comp}(G)=\delta_\infty=(3^s+1)\Delta(T)}
\]

for every quotient with nonzero compact image. No assertion that all specialized homology is compact, or that a general homology-tensor edge map is injective, is used.

## 5. Exact replacement ratios under any compatible double cover

Fix a universal abelian double cover

\[
0\longrightarrow\langle t\rangle\longrightarrow\widetilde U
\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow0.
\]

Lift the two actual terms of \(A\) to \(\widetilde X,\widetilde Y\), and normalize constants. The lifted checks become

\[
\widetilde A=1+\widetilde X+\widetilde Y,
\qquad
\widetilde B=1+H\widetilde X^q+K\widetilde Y^q.
\]

The torsion subgroup \(\widetilde T\) has order \(2|T|\), with \(H,K\) projecting to \(h,k\). Let

\[
J=\langle H,K\rangle\le\widetilde T.
\]

### 5.1 The deck element is not in the check-generated subgroup

If \(t\notin J\), projection identifies \(J\) with \(T\). The universal cover complex is a direct sum of two complexes isomorphic to the base complex, with a weight-preserving chain section of projection. A surviving base compact cycle therefore has a same-weight surviving lift in one component. Conversely, a universal nonboundary in the direct sum has at least the base universal minimum. Thus

\[
\boxed{\widetilde\delta_{\rm comp}=\delta_{\rm comp}.}
\]

Finite additional relations can connect the images of the two components. This does not affect the argument: projection of the chosen cycle is still the finite base nonboundary.

### 5.2 The deck element belongs to the check-generated subgroup

Then \(J=\widetilde T\), so the cover is again in the same normal form. Its odd torsion order is still \(o\). Because it is generated by two elements and has an order-two kernel over \(T\), its two-primary invariant factors must be either

\[
(2^{a+1},2^b)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(2^a,2^{b+1}).
\]

The latter case is possible only when \(b<a\). To see that these exhaust the options, the cover exponent is between the base exponent and twice that exponent, and its order is doubled; the second invariant factor is then determined.

Apply the exact compact-distance formula to the cover. It gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\widetilde\delta_{\rm comp}}{\delta_{\rm comp}}
=\begin{cases}
2,&(a,b)\mapsto(a+1,b),\\[2pt]
3/2,&(a,b)\mapsto(a,b+1).
\end{cases}}
\]

Here compact survival is needed to conclude that the cover's shortest universal generator survives the selected finite quotient. It follows from the supplied survival theorem; a self-contained reminder is given next.

### 5.3 Finite survival, including torsion collapse and nonsplit finite covers

Project to the odd norm sector. Odd coset-sum inclusion has projection as a left inverse, so it injects finite homology. If odd torsion collapses, every fiber has odd size and the norm specializes to the norm of its image. The odd subgroup has a unique odd-order lift; the deck cannot disappear under this reduction.

In the remaining two-primary normal form,

\[
B^M=A^{qM},\qquad Z=(A^{qM-1},B^{M-1})
\]

is a universal generator. A surviving compact class makes its finite image nontrivial. The three monomials of these powers remain distinct: a collision would make \(A\) or \(B\) a unit in the finite base ring.

The lifted powers differ by \(\epsilon D_0\), \(\epsilon=1+t\), where \(D_0\) is a subset of those three monomials. If the subset is empty or full, an appropriate canonical power cycle projects to \(Z\). Otherwise transfer of \(Z\) is a nonboundary. Indeed, a boundary preimage \(f\) would give both

\[
\epsilon D_0 f=0,\qquad \epsilon\widetilde A^{qM}f=0.
\]

One of \(D_0\) and \(D_0+\widetilde A^{qM}\) is a monomial unit. Hence \(\epsilon f=0\), and in any actual group-algebra double cover

\[
\operatorname{ann}(\epsilon)=\epsilon\widetilde R.
\]

Writing \(f=\epsilon r\) would project to a boundary preimage for \(Z\), a contradiction.

Thus some universal cover class survives. Since the new minimum cover cycle generates its whole compact module, that minimum cycle survives too. This argument only uses actual finite boundary equations. It is unaffected by even periods, torsion collapse, or relations identifying a free-period word with the deck element.

The resulting factor two is optimal for compact replacement.

## 6. Sharpness and examples

### 6.1 An exact finite sharpness example

Take \(A=B=1+x+y\) on \(C_3\times C_3\). Double the first period and take

\[
A'=1+x+y,\qquad B'=1+x^4+y
\]

on \(C_6\times C_3\). These are valid matched strict trinomials. Universally the base has trivial torsion, while the check-generated cover torsion is \(C_2\), with \(t=x^3\) in the finite quotient. Thus compact distances are exactly two and four.

The actual finite parameters are also

\[
[[18,4,2]]\longrightarrow[[36,4,4]].
\]

The base logical is \((1,1)\), and the cover logical is \((1+x^3,1+x^3)\). Their nonboundary certificates are direct. For instance map the cover ring to \(\mathbb F_4[\eta]/(\eta^2)\), with \(\omega^2+\omega+1=0\), by

\[
x\mapsto\omega(1+\eta),\qquad y\mapsto\omega^2.
\]

Then \(A'\mapsto\omega\eta\), \(B'\mapsto0\), and the logical maps to \((\eta,\eta)\); its nonzero first component precludes a boundary. Every cover check column has weight three and all 36 columns are distinct, so there is no weight-two cycle and no odd cycle. This establishes distance four. Exact matrix ranks give dimension four on each side. The verification script includes these rank and distinct-column checks.

This is not a new amplification record. It certifies optimality of the factor two within the compact normal-form theorem.

### 6.2 Why the old generator suggested a false superlinear cost

For trivial odd torsion and \(q=1\), consider

\[
T=C_M,\qquad T'=C_M\times C_2,\qquad M=2^a\ge2.
\]

The base compact distance is \(2M\), and the cover compact distance is \(3M\). The unadjusted cover power generator has weight \(2\cdot3^a\), which can exceed \(3\cdot2M\) for large \(a\). That is not a counterexample: the norm-adjusted generator has exact weight \(3M\).

For example, with \(M=8\), the base compact minimum is 16. The old cover generator has weight 54, apparently larger than the proposed threshold 48, but a boundary adjustment gives the true compact minimum 24. The ratio is \(3/2\), not \(54/16\).

## 7. Implications for actual BB double covers and the global bounds

Take a finite minimum logical and its connected incidence presentation. The supplied framework ensures that its universal lift has the same weight \(d\) and remains nontrivial in the finite base. Therefore

\[
\delta_{\rm comp}=d
\]

for that presentation: the chosen lift gives the upper bound, and any lighter surviving universal cycle would give a lighter finite logical.

If that presentation is in the genuinely higher-rank non-finite-check regime, the normal-form theorem and the new result give

\[
\boxed{d_2\le2d.}
\]

Therefore a finite ratio larger than two cannot have a minimum logical governed by this regime. At least one of the exclusions must apply: the presentation has free rank zero or one, or a check is supported in a finite subgroup. This statement does not classify all such exceptional codes.

The supplied incidence-size bounds remain:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{presentation type}&\text{cover bound}\\\hline
\text{finite}&2d^4\\
\text{free rank one}&8d^3\\
\text{higher rank with a finite-support check}&2d^2\\
\text{genuinely higher rank}&2d\quad\text{(new)}
\end{array}
\]

Using the same external arbitrary-boundary lattice-distance theorem as the previous round improves the finite-presentation row to at most \(32d^3\). Consequently the all-code fixed-distance bound improves from the preceding \(32d^{\log_2 9}\) to

\[
\boxed{d_2\le32d^3.}
\]

The elementary \(2d^4\) bound also remains available. The square-root truncated-size bound

\[
\Gamma(N)\le24\sqrt N
\]

is unchanged, since its worst cases were the low-rank ones. These global corollaries use the supplied presentation estimates and, for the cubic bound, Arnault et al., arXiv:2502.04995v2, Theorem 3.6. They are not independent re-proofs of those earlier estimates.

No finite numerical upper bound on \(C_*\) follows. The best exact lower bound in the supplied research state is still \(7/3\), attained by the established connected family. No new exact ratio above that value is claimed here. The universal thresholds \(7/3\), \(5/2\), and \(3\) remain unsettled for the full class, although factor two is now proved throughout the targeted compact normal form.

## 8. Adversarial checks and what they certify

The main theorem is symbolic. None of its lower bounds comes from a search that failed to find a short vector.

`verify_sparse_generators.py` checks 114 universal polynomial cases, including unequal two-primary invariant factors, nontrivial slopes, several Frobenius powers, and odd torsion. It verifies exact cyclehood, the exact weight formula, and an explicitly reconstructed universal boundary between the optimized and canonical generators.

`verify_embeddings.py` uses a separate integer-polynomial implementation of the generic evaluation into a twisted group algebra. It checks linear independence of 440 reduced monomials in eight testbeds and 640 random exact polynomial instances. It verifies the exact kernel behavior against division by B, without finite-field specialization. These are supplemental finite checks of the symbolic coset argument, not its proof.

`field_distance.py` independently builds the classical finite-field check matrices and exhaustively enumerates their kernels in 15 F_4 testbeds. All agree with the kernel-distance formula. The largest tested kernel has dimension eight on 64 coordinates and exact minimum weight 27. These are classical kernel distances, not quantum-code distances. An additional negative control on \(C_3\times C_3\) has distance six at a nontrivial \(\mathbb F_4\) coefficient, whereas its generic transcendental kernel distance is nine. This explicitly confirms that the generic coefficient hypothesis cannot be replaced by arbitrary finite-field evaluation in the presence of odd torsion.

`verify_finite_replacement.py` tests 2,880 matched bivariate-cover parameter instances. There are 1,161 certified pairs with nonzero compact base generator: 337 have predicted compact ratio 1, 204 have ratio 3/2, and 620 have ratio 2. They include 729 even-coordinate covers, 144 odd-norm cases, and nine explicitly constructed torsion-collapse cases in which an entire universal torsion generator is killed by the finite quotient. Some counts overlap.

`audit_replacement_certificates.py` is independent of the construction and rank routines. It rebuilds the checks, tests the exact cycle equations, tests each separating dual functional against every boundary, computes ranks with the opposite pivot convention, checks term matching, and independently reconstructs the optimized norm images. It verifies 2,322 primal/dual certificates.

The finite physical weights may be smaller than the universal formula because finite coordinates collide. The formula is not being used as a lower bound on finite quantum distance. The only exact finite quantum distances separately asserted in this report are the small sharpness example in Section 6.1; those have explicit lower checks.

These verifications are not proof-assistant formalization and are not an independent human referee report. No literature-priority claim is made.

## 9. Failed routes, resolved obstruction, and the next lemma

The power-generator estimate was the main misleading route. It contains a fractal factor in the larger torsion exponent, even when that factor can be replaced by a subgroup norm. In an exponent-preserving double cover it can suggest ratios above three. The optimized generator and the universal lower bound show that this distortion belongs to the choice of representative, not to compact homology.

A one-sided torsion lower bound was insufficient: it only bounds the second block. The faithful generic B-evaluation adds roots of the torsion generators and exposes the missing \(3^s\) contribution in the first block. This is why the final result compares the exact physical sum of both block weights.

No generic finite specialization-injectivity theorem is used. Generic evaluation is used only in the universal lower bound, with its kernel proved explicitly. Actual finite survival is obtained from the universal generator identity and the direct power-cycle nonboundary argument. Specialization-created homology remains allowed and can only reduce the finite distance relative to the exhibited compact witness.

The targeted higher-rank metric obstruction is therefore resolved. The next obstruction is genuinely different: one-dimensional Laurent factorization and cancellation, or finite-support mechanisms, rather than the torsion–Frobenius normal form.

**Single next target: the rank-one actual-logical replacement lemma.** Let

\[
S=\mathbb F_2[T][z^{\pm1}],
\]

with T finite, and let two strict trinomials generate a connected rank-one presentation. For every compatible finite quotient with nonzero compact base image and every matched double cover, prove or refute

\[
\boxed{d_2\le3\,\delta_{\rm comp}.}
\]

The conclusion permits all finite cover logicals, not only compact ones or transferred ones. For a minimum-circuit presentation, \(\delta_{\rm comp}=d\). Begin with cyclic-free base torsion \(T=1\), where the universal base cycle module is explicitly the primitive syzygy of two Laurent trinomials and the established 7/3 family already lives. Its short representative comes from non-Frobenius univariate cancellations, so the new two-dimensional distance formula cannot be applied to it.

This would settle the factor-three milestone for the rank-one minimum-circuit regime; rank-zero and finite-check linear bounds would still require separate arguments. It is not being presented as an already sufficient proof of a universal constant for every BB code.

The dyadic support argument is related in spirit to Sierpinski-pattern classical codes; B. Yoshida, *Information storage capacity of discrete spin systems*, arXiv:1111.3275v3, is relevant context. No theorem from that paper is imported into the proof here.
