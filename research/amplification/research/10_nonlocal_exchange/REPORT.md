# Nonlocal tree exchanges at primitive slope (2,1)

## STATUS: CONSTANT_BOUND_STILL_OPEN

The full compact inequality `widehat Delta <= 3 Delta` and the stronger half-weight completion lemma are neither proved nor refuted here. No counterexample to the complete compact minimum and no new actual amplification ratio is claimed. The inherited factor-four bound for this slope is unchanged.

The strongest new result is a **complete nonlocal replacement of the source-free boundary variables by a finite independent-set optimization**, for every homology message. It retains the optimization over all messages. Two sparse local identities characterize the common-message constraint exactly, so arbitrary signed-graph counterexamples need not be mistaken for BB examples.

Two exact negative controls identify limitations of potential proofs. First, three simultaneously eligible tree components can form a triangle in genuine common-message data, despite bipartiteness of the original graph. Second, a fixed BB example with base compact minimum six has polynomial messages in one fixed universal cover class whose exact boundary-field minima are 74N, for every positive odd N, while the minimum over that full universal class is fourteen. Thus minimum base weight plus fixed-polynomial-message optimality cannot justify a uniform tree-exchange argument. The latter is a family of representatives, not an amplification family.

## 1. Inherited setting and the full optimization

Work in P=F_2[z,z^{-1}], with

\[
X=z^r,\quad Y=z^{2s},\quad H=z^{2g},\qquad r\text{ odd},\quad g>0,
\]
\[
A=1+X+Y,\qquad B=1+XH^2+YH.
\]

The checks are strict and their normalized differences are primitive. Put

\[
e=\gcd(A,1+z^g),\quad j=\gcd(A,1+XH),\quad c=ej,
\]
\[
a_0=A/c,\quad b_0=B/c,\quad G=e^2j=\gcd(A^2,A+B).
\]

A is square-free, because its Laurent derivative is a monomial. In particular e and j are coprime, and a_0 is coprime to c. Let E_0 be a nonconstant divisor of e, the compact divisor of the selected hard-core finite specialization. The base compact minimum is

\[
\Delta=\min_{E_0\nmid f}(|b_0f|+|a_0f|).
\]

These are universal finite-support weights. They are not weights after identifying sites in a finite quotient.

The cover checks are A+epsilon, B+epsilon, where epsilon=1+t and t^2=1. Define

\[
P_0=(AB+A+B)/G,\quad S_0=(A+B)/G,\quad Q_0=A^2/G.
\]

The supplied exact full parameterization, also checked algebraically in this round, is

\[
C(\phi,h)=\bigl(P_0\phi+(B+1)h,\ S_0\phi+h,
                  Q_0\phi+(A+1)h,\ h\bigr),\qquad\phi,h\in P.
\]

Its homology coordinate is phi modulo G. A universal boundary generated in epsilon coordinates by l+epsilon k has parameter change

\[
\delta\phi=Gk,\qquad\delta h=l+Ak.
\]

The finite compact image is P/(E_0), so C(phi,h) survives exactly when E_0 does not divide phi. Thus

\[
\widehat\Delta=\min_{\substack{\phi,h\in P\\E_0\nmid\phi}}|C(\phi,h)|.
\]

No universal homology component is removed from this minimization. The existence of additional specialization-created finite classes is not excluded or used to certify these universal weights.

Let

\[
\ell=r-2s,\quad m=\ell+2g,\quad K=1+z^\ell,\quad J=1+z^m,
\quad\theta=Y^{-2}H^{-1}.
\]

Both graph steps are odd and nonzero. Put

\[
\alpha=(YH)^{-1}P_0\phi,\quad
\beta=Y^{-1}Q_0\phi,\quad\sigma=S_0\phi.
\]

Then

\[
\mathcal E_\phi(h)=|\alpha+Jh|+|\beta+Kh|+|\sigma+h|+|h|.
\]

The graph has edges of type m from i to i-m with sign alpha_i, and edges of type ell from i to i-ell with sign beta_i. Parallel edges are retained as distinct edges. Write F for the sign-one edges and S= supp(sigma) for the source vertices. For a spin support W,

\[
\mathcal E_\phi(W)=|F\mathbin\triangle\delta W|+|S|+2|W\setminus S|.
\]

Thus source spins have constant unary cost one, and every occupied nonsource has unary cost two.

## 2. Two local identities are the COMPLETE common-message condition

In addition to the inherited square-parity relation, there is a second sparse identity:

\[
\boxed{K\alpha+J\beta=\theta\sigma,\qquad
H\alpha+\beta+K\sigma=0.}
\]

The first follows from

\[
(A+1)(AB+A+B)+(B+1)A^2=A+B.
\]

For the second,

\[
AB+A+B+A^2=(A+1)(A+B).
\]

More strongly, the two identities are sufficient: every finite Laurent triple satisfying them is generated by one polynomial message phi as in Section 1.

For a proof, consider

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}K&J&\theta\\H&1&K\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It has rank two over the fraction field, since K+HJ=(A+B)/Y is nonzero. Its vector of maximal minors is

\[
(KJ+\theta,\ K^2+H\theta,\ K+HJ)
=\frac GY\bigl((YH)^{-1}P_0,\ Y^{-1}Q_0,\ S_0\bigr).
\]

The vector on the right is primitive over the Laurent PID: gcd(Q_0,S_0)=1. Every element of ker(M) is a rational multiple of this vector, and primitivity forces that rational multiple to be a Laurent polynomial. This proves the claimed equality of modules.

Furthermore,

\[
Y^{-1}Q_0=Y^{-1}j a_0^2
\]

is a unit modulo E_0. Hence

\[
E_0\nmid\phi\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E_0\nmid\beta.
\]

The full admissible graph data can therefore be described without an unspecified message:

\[
M(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)^T=0,\qquad E_0\nmid\beta.
\]

This exactly retains the common-message restriction. The square-parity equation alone is not enough.

## 3. A sharper support bound for each fixed message

**Proposition.** Every minimum-energy field lies in the ordinary interval hull of the source vertices and the endpoints of sign-one edges.

Let that anchor interval be [L_-,L_+]. Suppose an occupied vertex v lies to the right of L_+, and choose the rightmost occupied vertex. It has no source and all four incident edge signs are zero. At least two incident edges go to greater vertices, whose spins are zero. Thus at least two incident edges are unsatisfied. Removing v changes energy by

\[
4-2u_v-2=2-2u_v\le-2,
\]

where u_v is the number of unsatisfied incident edges. This contradicts optimality. The left endpoint is identical.

This argument counts parallel edges correctly and works for negative steps. Empty anchor data have the unique minimum field zero. It strictly improves the prior support-neighborhood bound, but does not bound the support of the message itself.

In particular, the fixed-message boundary problem has an exact finite domain, with no heuristic support cutoff. A transfer-matrix computation on that interval, with zero exterior spins, covers all finite Laurent boundary fields.

## 4. All occupied nonsource variables form canonical trees

Fix the message and fix an arbitrary assignment x to the source vertices. Among energy-minimizing fields with that source assignment, choose one with the least number of occupied nonsources.

At an occupied nonsource vertex v, removal changes energy by 2-2u_v. If u_v>=1, removal either decreases energy or preserves energy and decreases support. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\text{Every edge incident on an occupied nonsource is satisfied.}}
\]

Now delete the source vertices from the occupied support and take a connected component R. Every edge incident to R is satisfied. Removing R changes energy by

\[
|\delta R|-2|R|=2(|R|-|E(R)|)=2(1-b_1(R)).
\]

It must be positive by the minimum-support choice. Since R is connected, b_1(R)>=0. Hence

\[
\boxed{R\text{ is a tree, and its complete removal costs exactly two.}}
\]

This applies also to source-free branches inside a component that contains source vertices. It is stronger than the statement concerning wholly source-free support components alone.

Every internal edge of R has sign zero. If a zero-sign edge connects R to another nonsource, that neighbor is forced to be occupied and lies in the same component. Thus R is an entire finite component of the zero-sign graph after deleting all source vertices. Infinite components cannot be occupied in a finite field. A zero-sign component with any internal extra edge, including a sign-one chord, cannot be occupied.

Consequently the candidate components are determined by the message, BEFORE choosing the source assignment:

1. Delete the source vertices and take components of zero-sign edges.
2. Discard infinite components and components connected to the zero exterior of the anchor interval.
3. Retain only components whose full induced multigraph is a tree and all of whose internal edges have sign zero.
4. A cut edge from R to a source v demands x_v=1+F_e. Inconsistent demands at one source discard the candidate.

Cut edges to other nonsources necessarily have sign one. These latter edges give incompatibilities between candidate trees.

## 5. Exact nonlocal contraction and independent-set formula

### 5.1 Truth table for one tree

Let R be any finite induced tree with no sources and zero internal edge signs. Hold every exterior spin fixed. Each edge leaving R prescribes the exterior value for which its inside endpoint would prefer to be one.

Relative to setting all spins of R to zero, the exact optimum is

\[
\boxed{-2\ \text{if every boundary requirement is met, and }0\ \text{otherwise}.}
\]

For a direct proof, let T be any nonempty occupied subset of R. Let c(T) be its number of induced components, d(T) the number of edges from T to R\\T, and b(T) the number of exterior cut requirements it violates. The improvement over the all-zero field is exactly

\[
2\bigl(c(T)-d(T)-b(T)\bigr).
\]

If T is a proper subset, every one of its components has at least one edge to R\\T, so d(T)>=c(T), and it cannot improve the energy. For T=R, the improvement is 2-2b(R). This proves the truth table, including arbitrary simultaneous exterior assignments.

Tree size therefore does not multiply its energy benefit: an eligible tree of any size contributes precisely one two-unit improvement. Size still affects the baseline edge cost and the boundary requirements; this is not a distance bound.

### 5.2 Complete elimination of the source-free field

Let Gamma_phi be the graph whose vertices are the candidate trees, joining two whenever an original edge connects them. All such edges have sign one. For a source assignment x, retain only candidates whose source requirements are met; call the induced graph Gamma_{phi,x}. Let iota denote its independence number.

Two adjacent candidates cannot both occur in a minimum-support optimum: their joining sign-one edge would be unsatisfied. Conversely any independent set of eligible candidates produces a valid field, each selected tree reducing the energy from the source-only field by exactly two. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\min_h\mathcal E_\phi(h)
=|\sigma|+\min_{x\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\sigma}
\left(|F\mathbin\triangle\delta x|-2\iota(\Gamma_{\phi,x})\right).
}
\]

This is the complete boundary-field minimum for every message, not a local heuristic and not merely a necessary optimality condition. Computing the independence number remains a global optimization.

Combining with Section 2 gives the following exact form of the full compact minimum:

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\Delta=
\min_{\substack{M(\alpha,\beta,\sigma)^T=0\\E_0\nmid\beta}}
\left[|\sigma|+
\min_{x\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\sigma}
\left(|F\mathbin\triangle\delta x|-2\iota(\Gamma_{\phi,x})\right)\right].
}
\]

Here phi is uniquely recovered from the primitive kernel vector if it is needed to name the graph. No sector is purified, and every message and both universal-boundary coefficients remain included.

## 6. The conflict graph need not be bipartite

Bipartiteness of the original two-step graph does not make the tree-conflict graph bipartite. This fails within genuine common-message data, not just in arbitrary signed graphs.

Use the record checks

\[
X=z,\quad Y=z^{-4},\quad H=z^6,\quad
A=1+z+z^{-4},\quad B=1+z^2+z^{13},
\]

with graph steps 5 and 11 and E_0=1+z+z^2. Take

\[
\phi=z^{-6}+z^4+z^8+z^{14}+z^{25}.
\]

It has nonzero residue modulo E_0. The message-generated edge data have three candidate trees

\[
T_1=\{0\},\qquad T_2=\{5\},\qquad T_3=\{11,16\}.
\]

All vertices of these trees are nonsources. Their sole internal tree edge, 11--16, has sign zero. Every edge leaving each tree has sign one. Thus all three are eligible with every source spin zero.

The conflict edges are 0--5, 0--11, and 5--16, giving a triangle. On the underlying square 0--5--16--11--0 there are exactly three sign-one edges. The square-parity identity forces sigma_14=1; that shifted source is not a vertex of the three tree components and does not prevent their simultaneous eligibility.

An independent local certificate checks all fifteen distinct incident-edge conditions needed for the three trees, both complete common-message identities, and nonzero compact residue. This is a counterexample to the proposed bipartite-conflict shortcut, NOT a distance counterexample. No minimality of the five-term message is claimed.

## 7. Minimum base weight does not control an arbitrarily fixed message

The next construction tests the quantifiers needed in a base-shortening argument. It stays in the same checks as Section 6.

### 7.1 The base minimum is exactly six

After gauging the first check by z^4, put

\[
A_*=1+z^4+z^5,\quad B_*=B,
\]
\[
b_*=B_*/A_*=1+z^2+z^4+z^5+z^6+z^7+z^8.
\]

All universal base cycles in this gauge are (b_*f,f). The weight-six nonboundary is, in the original coordinates,

\[
z_{\min}=(z^5+z^{10}+z^{14}+z^{15},\ z+z^3).
\]

Its primitive base message is 1 modulo E_0.

For completeness, weights below six are excluded symbolically. Every cycle has even weight. A one-term f gives weight eight because b_* has weight seven. If f has three terms, b_*f cannot be a monomial: b_* is a nonunit. If f has two terms and b_*f also has two terms, monomial gauges give

\[
b_*(1+z^n)=1+z^m.
\]

Then n divides m and b_* must be the geometric quotient with m/n terms and degree m-n. Weight seven and degree eight would require 8=6n, impossible. All other support counts below six are excluded by nonzero product and parity. Thus Delta=6 without a support cutoff.

The inherited exact compact cover minimum is fourteen. Its unrestricted-length closure certificate is included and re-audited in this bundle. A particular weight-fourteen word in the original coordinates has

\[
U_0=\{30,37,38\},\quad U_1=\{27,31,33,34,36,38,39\},
\]
\[
V_0=\{23\},\quad V_1=\{25,26,27\},
\]

and universal message

\[
\phi_*=z^{29}+z^{32}+z^{33}.
\]

### 7.2 Exact fixed-message minimum 74

Define phi_0 and h_0 by

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{supp}\phi_0=\{&-15,-5,-2,-1,0,4,8,9,13,14,16,17,18,20,\\
&28,29,30,34,35,41,44,45\},
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\operatorname{supp}h_0=
\{-19,-18,-14,-13,-4,0,5,6,10,13,15,20,25,28,38\}.
\]

The complete fixed-polynomial-message boundary problem has

\[
\boxed{\min_h\mathcal E_{\phi_0}(h)=74,}
\]

and among its optima the least field support is fifteen, attained by h_0. This minimizes h over ALL finite Laurent supports, but does not allow changes in phi_0. Its source-only field has energy 82, and its four eligible selected trees save eight units. The free trees are

\[
\{-13\},\quad\{0,5,10,15,20,25\},\quad\{6\},\quad\{38\}.
\]

There are 24 source vertices. The attaining source assignment is

\[
\{-19,-18,-14,-4,13,28\}.
\]

The compact residue is nonzero, and a separately reconstructed finite group at L=501 has a dual functional detecting this actual cycle while annihilating all 1002 boundary generators.

The conditional minimum is certified by Bellman lower potentials on the proved anchor interval [-30,51], followed by eleven forced zero spins. The cost is lexicographic: 83 times physical energy plus field support. An independent standard-library auditor checks all 358400 Bellman inequalities and the attaining path, proving the value 74 and the support minimum 15.

This is explicitly NOT a claim that the complete compact minimum is 74.

### 7.3 The same universal class has minimum fourteen

Here

\[
G=e^2j=1+z+z^2+z^4+z^7,
\]

and

\[
\phi_0\equiv\phi_*\equiv1+z^3+z^4+z^5\pmod G.
\]

Thus both cycles are in the same UNIVERSAL homology class, not merely the same finite compact class. Set

\[
k_0=(\phi_0+\phi_*)/G,\qquad
l_0=h_0+(z^{25}+z^{26}+z^{27})+Ak_0.
\]

Their difference is exactly the universal cover boundary generated by l_0+epsilon k_0. The auditor verifies the division and all four physical-coordinate identities.

The direct base boundary from the six-vertex tree T={0,5,10,15,20,25} is (B 1_T,A 1_T), of weight 26. Adding it to z_min gives weight 26, not a shortening. Global base minimality already excludes EVERY weight-reducing specialization-null base move, so an argument from fixed-message tree optimality alone cannot produce one.

### 7.4 A uniform separated-message family

For each positive odd N, set

\[
T_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}z^{210j},\qquad
\phi_N=\phi_0 T_N,\qquad h_N=h_0 T_N.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\min_h\mathcal E_{\phi_N}(h)=74N,}
\]

with minimum field support 15N, while the minimum over their fixed universal homology class is fourteen.

The equality of classes follows from z^210=1 modulo G and odd N. The attaining words have disjoint translates, so the upper values 74N and 15N are immediate.

For the lower bound, each translated signal has anchors [-30+210j,51+210j]. Between consecutive signals, the eleven vertices 75+210j through 85+210j lie in zero-sign four-cycles with corners i,i+5,i+11,i+16; all their vertices are nonsources. An occupied nonsource in a minimum-support optimum forces occupancy along every zero edge. A resulting zero-edge cycle is impossible by Section 4. Hence all eleven separator spins are zero.

Since the largest edge step is eleven, these zero slices separate the optimization into independent single-signal problems. The extremal-vertex argument removes any excess field outside each signal's own anchor interval. Each block costs at least 74 and, at that energy, requires at least fifteen field sites. This proves both lower bounds uniformly.

The entire universal class has a weight-fourteen representative because phi_N-phi_* is divisible by G. The inherited global compact lower bound fourteen applies to that nonzero class. Thus the class minimum is exactly fourteen for all odd N.

This disproves the assertion that global minimality of a base representative, together with minimum-support optimality of a boundary field at an arbitrary compatible polynomial message, controls the total tree cost. It does NOT refute an assertion requiring joint optimality over messages, the half-weight lemma, or factor three. These are representatives in one fixed code, not a parametric amplification construction.

## 8. The exact nonlocal information supplied by base minimality

Let z=(b_0 f_0,a_0 f_0) attain Delta, with E_0 not dividing f_0. Global minimality gives, for EVERY tau in P,

\[
\boxed{
2|\operatorname{supp}z\cap\operatorname{supp}N(\tau)|
\le |N(\tau)|,
\quad N(\tau)=(b_0 E_0\tau,a_0 E_0\tau).
}
\]

The reason is that adding N(tau) leaves the compact message f_0 nonzero modulo E_0. This is stronger than testing universal base boundaries (Bq,Aq), which are obtained by tau=(c/E_0)q. N(tau) may be a universal nonboundary that dies under the chosen finite specialization. Using these allowed null directions is not sector-first purification.

For a cover move preserving the selected compact class, write

\[
\delta\phi=E_0\lambda,\qquad\delta h=\mu.
\]

Its projected base message changes by

\[
\delta f_{\rm proj}
=(A/e)E_0\lambda+c\mu
=jE_0\bigl(a_0\lambda+(e/E_0)\mu\bigr).
\]

Since gcd(a_0,e/E_0)=1, these are exactly the jE_0-multiple base messages. The kernel of this projection is parameterized by

\[
\lambda=(e/E_0)\nu,\qquad\mu=a_0\nu,
\]

and gives the compact transfer of (b_0 nu,a_0 nu), which vanishes in the hard-core compact finite image. Thus all such filling changes are allowed and must remain in the optimization.

A tree removal with lambda=0 and mu=1_R projects to the ordinary base boundary (B 1_R,A 1_R). Its cover cost is exactly two in the canonical situation. But this alone gives no overlap inequality with the ORIGINAL minimum z. The explicit example in Section 7 makes that failure concrete.

## 9. Remaining precise obstruction

Every fixed-message source-free field is now replaced by compatible two-unit tree rewards. Tree lengths still affect the baseline signs, source data, and compatibility requirements; they have not been bounded in terms of Delta.

The unresolved statement is

\[
\boxed{
\min_{E_0\nmid\phi}\left[
|\sigma|+\min_{x\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\sigma}
\bigl(|F\mathbin\triangle\delta x|-2\iota(\Gamma_{\phi,x})\bigr)
\right]\le3\Delta.
}
\]

There are two important restrictions on a next proof. Fixed-message optimality cannot replace the outer minimum, even when the universal class is held fixed; Section 7 gives an unbounded exact failure. Also the conflict graph cannot be treated as bipartite; Section 6 gives a four-site common-message obstruction.

The most promising next lemma is a **packing-deficit transfer lemma**: at a JOINTLY optimal message and source assignment, a value above 3 Delta should yield a finite Laurent tau with

\[
2|\operatorname{supp}z\cap\operatorname{supp}N(\tau)|>|N(\tau)|.
\]

That would contradict the exact global-minimality inequalities of Section 8. The missing construction must use both sparse common-message identities and the globally optimal tree packing, and must be allowed to change the message by E_0 lambda. Producing tau from a tree's size alone is not justified.

No such construction is proved here. A genuine refutation still requires a lower certificate for the FULL compact minimum above 3 Delta. By the supplied large-period realization theorem it would then give actual finite-distance amplification above three; the conditional minima 74N do not meet that requirement.

The graph and forest theorems extend to any two nonzero steps, without bipartiteness. For other primitive slopes, the common-message identities use the ratio of the two even monomials in place of H, with the corresponding graph steps. This transfers the structural reduction, not a factor-three theorem.
