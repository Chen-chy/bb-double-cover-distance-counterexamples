\begin{abstract}
Binary bivariate bicycle codes with three distinct monomials in each check need not obey either of the proposed double-cover distance inequalities $d\le d_2\le2d$. We give connected, term-matched covers with exact parameters $\Qcode{78}{4}{6}\to\Qcode{156}{4}{14}$ and $\Qcode{156}{4}{14}\to\Qcode{312}{8}{12}$. Explicit logical operators and two independent exhaustive circuit enumerations certify both examples. We then prove the replacement inequalities $d\le4d_2^3$ and $d_2\le8d^3$, for every positive-dimensional strict-trinomial binary abelian double cover. The upper constant follows from an index-two transport of incidence relation lattices, combining the two previously separate proof directions. The proof treats arbitrary finite-support circuits, repeated roots, torsion collapse, and finite homology not represented by surviving compact classes. A self-contained cleaning and geometry-of-numbers argument also gives an explicit $O(n^{3/4})$ ambient distance estimate and $O(\sqrt N)$ truncated distance-distortion bounds. Projection and transfer identify the mechanisms behind the counterexamples; in particular, $k_2=2k$ still implies $d_2\le2d$. All claimed finite distances are accompanied by regenerable, independently checked certificates. Neither a universal linear amplification bound nor a positive universal degradation ratio is asserted.
\end{abstract}
